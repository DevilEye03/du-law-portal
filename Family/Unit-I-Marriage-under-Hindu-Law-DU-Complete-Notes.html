Concept of Marriage in General & Nature of Hindu MarriageSYLLABUS PHRASE 1 • SACRAMENT vs CONTRACT vs STATUS • 8 CLASSICAL FORMS

Why examiners love this: it opens every “validity” answer and short-notes. DU expects Mayne + Mulla + Lexis formulation.

1.1 Marriage in General — Universal Concept

Lexis Family Law Lectures Ch.1 (p.1–3, in your uploads): “Marriage is the foundation of a stable family; it accords status and security to parties and offspring. All legal systems provide for certain conditions for a valid marriage.” Three juristic lenses:

LensMeaningHindu law position
ContractConsensual union creating rights/duties; dissolubleNot pure contract — consent now relevant (Sec 5(ii), 12(1)(c)), but no offer-acceptance like Nikah; minors could marry pre-1955 via guardians (factum valet).
Sacrament (Samskara)Sacred, indissoluble religious tie; dharma, praja, ratiClassical view: one of 10 samskaras, only samskara for Shudras/women; saptapadi creates indissoluble bond. Still quoted in Pinninti (Madras texts) & Seema (Rigvedic ideal).
StatusLegal condition conferring legitimacy, maintenance, successionModern codified view: HMA creates a statutory status — Sec 11/12/16/18 fix consequences; divorce (Sec 13) makes it dissoluble. Hence hybrid.
Exam line (mug this): “Under classical Hindu law marriage was a sacrament; under HMA 1955 it retains sacramental solemnisation (Sec 7) but consequences are statutory — monogamy, age, void/voidable, divorce — hence it is best described as sacrament + contract + status.” — Paras Diwan; approved in DU lectures.

1.2 Classical Aims & 8 Forms (Smriti Background — Sec 4 saves only what HMA doesn’t cover)

Three aims: Dharma (religious duty), Praja (progeny, esp. son for sraddha), Rati (companionship/pleasure). Eight forms in Manu (only Brahma & Asura discussed in Lokhande p.615 Mulla):

Approved (Dharmya) — 4Unapproved (Adharmya) — 4HMA relevance today
Brahma (gift to learned groom), Daiva, Arsha, PrajapatyaAsura (bride-price), Gandharva (love union), Rakshasa (capture), Paishacha (seduction)HMA does not preserve forms; any two Hindus may marry if Sec 5 + Sec 7 satisfied. But Lokhande holds even Gandharva needs invocation + saptapadi unless custom abrogates. Forms now only evidentiary for “customary rites”.
Sec 4 overriding effect: Any Smriti text/custom inconsistent with HMA ceases. So child-marriage sacramental validity (factum valet) survives only because HMA Sec 11/12 deliberately omit age — see Pinninti full bench reasoning (Sec 4 + Sec 5 scheme).

1.4 Nature — Case-law Anchors (quote in answers)

  • Venkatacharyulu v. Rangacharyulu (1891) 14 Mad 316 (cited in Pinninti): “Saptapadi creates religious tie which once created cannot be untied; minor or unsound mind does not prevent valid samskara.” → Use for sacrament point.
  • Sivanandy v. Bhagavathyamma AIR 1962 Mad 400 (cited Pinninti): Minor’s marriage valid; doctrine of factum valet — quod fieri non debuit, factum valet (what ought not be done, yet done, is valid).
  • Lata Singh v. State of UP (2006) 5 SCC 475 (Lexis p.5): Right to marry person of choice = Art 21; inter-caste marriage legal, in national interest.
  • X v. Hospital Z (1998) 8 SCC 296 & (2003) 1 SCC 500 (Lexis p.4–5): Right to marry not absolute; suspended during communicable disease; but with informed consent no bar — balances sacrament with public health.
  • Lily Thomas / Sarla Mudgal line: “Marriage under every personal law is sacred; under Hindu law a sacrament — both to be preserved; religion not a commodity to exploit.” (DU p.19, para 39).

Sec 2 HMA — Applicability: Who is a Hindu?SYLLABUS: “APPLICABILITY OF LEGISLATION (SEC 2)” • LEAD CASE: SURAJMANI STELLA KUJUR

The threshold question in every validity/bigamy problem. If HMA doesn’t apply, Sec 5/11/17 don’t apply at all.

Bare Act — Sec 2 (summarised, HMA pp.3–4 in uploads) Sec 2(1)(a): Hindu by religion in any form/development — Virashaiva, Lingayat, Brahmo, Prarthana, Arya Samaj.
Sec 2(1)(b): Buddhist, Jaina, Sikh by religion.
Sec 2(1)(c): Any other person domiciled in India who is not Muslim, Christian, Parsi, Jew — unless proved not governed by Hindu law/custom before Act.
Explanation: Child (legitimate/illegitimate) of Hindu parents; or one Hindu parent + brought up as Hindu; convert/reconvert to Hindu/Buddhist/Jaina/Sikh.
Sec 2(2): Nothing applies to Scheduled Tribes (Art 366(25)) unless Centre notifies.
Sec 2(3): “Hindu” in Act includes all above persons.
Sec 1(2): Extends to whole India (J&K included since Act 34/2019) + Hindus domiciled in India but outside territories.

2.1 Clause-by-clause Deep Dive

A. Sec 2(1)(a) — Hindu by religion (no definition!)

  • No statutory definition of “Hindu” in HMA / Succession Act — Privy Council in Bhagwan Koer v. J.C. Bose (1903) 31 Cal 11 (quoted Surajmani para 4, DU p.1): “Hindu religion marvellously catholic and elastic… easier to say who are not Hindus.” Test is negative + community perception.
  • Includes Virashaiva/Lingayat/Arya Samaj etc. — reformist sects remain Hindu. Lingayat separate-religion claim rejected for HMA purposes.
  • Atheist/apostate Hindu? One who rejects rituals but not converted to Muslim/Christian/Parsi/Jew remains Hindu under 2(1)(c) (domicile + negative test).

B. Sec 2(1)(b) — Buddhist/Jaina/Sikh

  • Constitution Art 25 Expl. II deems them Hindus for temple-entry; HMA follows. PIL to exclude Sikhs rejected in 2010 (CJI Balakrishnan — Lexis p.6): matter for Government, not Court.
  • Jain-Buddhist customary marriages valid under Sec 7 if Sec 5 met.

C. Sec 2(1)(c) — Residuary: domicile + negative test

  • Two cumulative tests: (i) domiciled in India (Sondur Gopal v. Sondur Rajni AIR 2013 SC 2678: domicile, not residence/citizenship; US citizen domiciled in India still governed), (ii) not Muslim/Christian/Parsi/Jew.
  • Proviso-burden: defendant must prove he was not governed by Hindu law before 1955 (e.g., custom of tribe). Mere assertion insufficient.
  • Inter-religion marriages: Hindu + Christian/Muslim cannot marry under HMA (both must be Hindus per Sec 5 “between any two Hindus”). Remedy: Special Marriage Act 1954. DU 2011 Q1(a): Hindu male + Christian girl = void under HMA (see also Gullipilli Sowria Raj AIR 2009 SC 1085).
  • Conversion cases: Hindu at marriage → converts later: HMA still governs first marriage (Vilayat Raj v. Sunita AIR 1983 Del 351). Wife converts to Islam before Sec 11 petition: HMA petition fails (Bhavna Wasif). Person failing to prove conversion to Hinduism cannot claim HMA divorce (Arife v. Gopal Dutt 2010 Del — Lexis p.7).

D. Explanation — Children & Converts

E. Sec 2(2) — Scheduled Tribes excluded

  • Members of ST (Art 342 Order 1950 as amended) outside HMA unless Centre notifies under 2(2) — never generally notified.
  • Surajmani rule: Oraon + Santhal (both in Part XII ST Order) = deemed tribals; even if they “profess Hinduism”, Sec 2(2) excludes; governed by Santhal custom, not Sec 5/11/17/494 via HMA. Custom must be pleaded + proved ancient/certain/reasonable; book reference alone insufficient; custom cannot create criminal offence (Art 20).
  • Exam trap: Tribal second marriage → no 494 via Sec 17 unless prosecution proves (i) tribal custom prohibits bigamy + makes second marriage void, (ii) second marriage validly solemnised per that custom.

Sec 3 Definitions — Custom, Blood, Sapinda, Prohibited DegreesSYLLABUS: “CONDITIONS (SEC 3 AND 5)” • THE DICTIONARY FOR EVERY VALIDITY PROBLEM

DU problem-questions on uncle-niece / cousins / step-relations are solved only with Sec 3(f)(g) + Explanation. Learn tables by heart.

Sec 3(a) — Custom & Usage “Any rule continuously + uniformly observed for long time, obtaining force of law among Hindus in local area/tribe/community/group/family” — Provisos: (i) certain, not unreasonable, not opposed to public policy; (ii) family custom not discontinued.
Proof burden (Surajmani para 10, Ramalakshmi Ammal 14 MIA 570): Plead + prove by clear & unambiguous evidence; ancient, certain, reasonable, invariable; construed strictly (derogation from general law). Mere book / oral assertion fails.

3.2 Sapinda — Sec 3(f) (Sec 5(v))

Sec 3(f) Text (i) Sapinda relationship extends to 3rd generation (inclusive) through mother and 5th (inclusive) through father, counting the person concerned as generation 1, tracing upwards.
(ii) Two persons are sapindas if one is lineal ascendant of other within limits, OR they have a common lineal ascendant within limits with reference to each of them.

Sec 5 Conditions for Valid Hindu Marriage — The Heart of Unit I“A MARRIAGE MAY BE SOLEMNIZED BETWEEN ANY TWO HINDUS, IF…” • CLAUSES (i)–(v) • VIOLATION CHART

Every validity problem = Sec 5 test → Sec 11/12/18 consequence. Learn the 3–1–1 scheme: 3 void, 1 voidable, 1 valid-but-punishable.

Bare Act — Sec 5 (as amended 1976 + 1978 + 1999) (i) neither party has a spouse living at time of marriage.
(ii) at time of marriage, neither party — (a) incapable of giving valid consent due to unsoundness of mind; OR (b) though capable of consent, suffering from mental disorder of such kind/extent as to be unfit for marriage & procreation; OR (c) subject to recurrent attacks of insanity ~~or epilepsy~~ (“or epilepsy” deleted 1999).
(iii) bridegroom 21 years, bride 18 years at time of marriage (raised from 18/15 in 1978).
(iv) not within prohibited degrees (Sec 3(g)) unless custom permits each of them.
(v) not sapindas (Sec 3(f)) unless custom permits each of them.
(vi) guardian consent — OMITTED 1978. Sec 5 opening words “may” held directory, not mandatory — consequences only as Sec 11/12/18 provide (Pinninti).

4.1 Clause (i) — Monogamy (detailed in §7 Void/Bigamy)

  • Applies to both husband & wife; “spouse living” = valid subsisting marriage (not void, not dissolved by death/divorce/decree). If first marriage void ab initio, second is not bigamous (Pinninti CrMP 809/76 argument — failed because child marriage valid).
  • Second marriage during subsistence = void under Sec 11 + 17, punishable 494/495. Conversion does not dissolve first marriage (Sec 13 ground only) — Lily Thomas, Sarla Mudgal.
  • Tribals: Sec 17 inapplicable (Surajmani); custom must make second void.

4.3 Clause (iii) — Age: 21 / 18 (most litigated — Pinninti + Lajja + PCMA)

  • History: 1955: 18/15 → 1978 Amendment (Act 2/1978): 21/18; guardian clause (vi) deleted simultaneously (majority = 18, so bride major).
  • HMA consequence alone: Valid, neither void nor voidable — only Sec 18(a) punishment + girl’s option of puberty divorce 13(2)(iv) (married <15, repudiates after 15 but before 18). Full Bench Pinninti overruled P.A. Saramma (which had held void). Ratio: Sec 11 lists only (i)(iv)(v); Sec 12 lists only (ii)-related; expressio unius; Sec 13(2)(iv) would be otiose if void; Child Marriage Restraint Act 1929 never voided; factum valet; Sec 4 saves old law except where HMA expressly alters.
  • PCMA 2006 overlay (Lajja): Child = M<21/F<18; child marriage = voidable at child’s option (Sec 3, petition within 2 yrs of majority, via guardian/CMPO if minor); void only if (Sec 12) minor enticed/trafficked/sold/forced OR (Sec 14) violates injunction. Maintenance/residence to girl (Sec 4), children legitimate (Sec 6), cognizable/non-bailable, CM Prohibition Officers. HMA Sec 11/12 still unamended — anomaly noted DU p.55.
  • Lajja holdings (2012 (193) DLT 61, Full Bench): (1) Minor marriage under HMA = neither void nor voidable under HMA, but voidable under PCMA Sec 3 at child’s instance; (2) Husband not lawful guardian of minor wife; custody with parents/Nirmal Chhaya till majority, considering welfare + girl’s wishes; (3) FIR 363/376 not automatically quashed on girl’s “consent” — minority + facts (age, maturity, force) matter; ossification + school certificate weighed.

4.4 Clauses (iv) & (v) — Custom exception (“governing each of them”)

  • Custom must govern both parties (each). If boy’s custom permits but girl’s prohibits → barred.
  • Proof as Sec 3(a): ancient, certain, reasonable, continuous, not opposed to public policy + strict proof (Surajmani). South Indian mama-bhanji, cross-cousin (mother’s brother’s daughter) often permitted; North generally prohibited.
  • Violation → void Sec 11 + Sec 18(b). No annulment needed but decree declaratory available; children legitimate Sec 16.

Sec 7 Solemnisation + Live-in + Sec 114 Evidence Presumption“MAY BE SOLEMNIZED PER CUSTOMARY RITES OF EITHER PARTY” • SAPTAPADI • 7-A TN • BIGAMY PROOF

Second-most examined after Sec 5. Lokhande + Nagalingam are a mandatory pair in every bigamy answer.

Bare Act — Sec 7 Sec 7(1): Hindu marriage may be solemnized per customary rites & ceremonies of either party.
Sec 7(2): Where such rites include saptapadi (7 steps jointly before sacred fire), marriage complete & binding when 7th step taken.
State Amendments: Sec 7-A (Tamil Nadu, 1967; + Puducherry): Suyamariyathai / Seerthiruththa — declaration in understood language / garlanding / tying thali in presence of relatives/friends = valid, notwithstanding Sec 7. Retrospective validation; subject to other HMA provisions.

5.1 “Solemnized” = Celebrated with proper ceremonies in due form

  • Lokhande (AIR 1965 SC 1564, DU p.8): Shorter Oxford meaning; “merely going through ceremonies with intent to be taken as married” ≠ solemnized. Unless marriage solemnized, Sec 17 voidness + 494 do not trigger — “whoever marries” = “whoever marries validly”.
  • Essential ceremonies (Mulla 12th ed. p.605/615, quoted Lokhande): (1) Invocation before sacred fire (homa), (2) Saptapadi — for Brahma & Asura and equally Gandharva unless custom abrogates with definite evidence. Prosecution led no custom evidence; garlanding + forehead-touching held insufficient → acquittal.

Voidable Marriages — Sec 12 + Sec 16 LegitimacyVALID TILL ANNULLED • 4 GROUNDS • STRICT BARS • FRAUD JURISPRUDENCE (P. v. K. • BABUI • ASHA)

DU Cases 06, 08, 09 all on Sec 12(1)(c) fraud + (a) impotency. Learn pre- vs post-1976 fraud text.

Bare Act — Sec 12 (as amended 1976/78) Sec 12(1): Any marriage (before/after Act) voidable & may be annulled on: (a) respondent impotent & marriage not consummated due to impotence; (b) contravenes 5(ii) mental; (c) consent of petitioner (or guardian where required pre-1978) obtained by force OR fraud as to nature of ceremony OR as to material fact/circumstance concerning respondent; (d) respondent pregnant by other at marriage.
Sec 12(2)(a) bar for (c): No petition if (i) filed >1 yr after force ceased/fraud discovered, OR (ii) petitioner lived as husband/wife with full consent after discovery.
Sec 12(2)(b) bar for (d): Need (i) petitioner ignorant at marriage, (ii) proceedings within 1 yr of marriage (post-Act) / 1 yr of commencement (pre-Act), (iii) no consensual marital intercourse since discovery.

8.1 Ground-wise Deep Dive

(a) Impotency + non-consummation — P. v. K. (AIR 1982 Bom 400, DU p.56)

  • Test: Practical impossibility of normal consummation (Lord Penzance classic; Mody J. adopts). Two-fold: (i) impotent at time of marriage (persisting), (ii) marriage not consummated due to it. Either spouse may petition (here husband).
  • P. v. K. facts: 36M + 27F, 20-6-1976; wife refuses from night 1 (“no sex for 1 yr”), aversion; pilgrimage fails; Dr Bhatia 27-8-76: 2nd-degree uterine prolapse + torn hymen; husband alleges concealment + non-virginity + impotency. Wife denies, claims ignorance.
  • Holding: Wife’s evidence “completely unreliable”; husband + Dr Bhatia believed; prolapse needs manual manipulation, sight cools ardour → not normal intercourse; resistance to conceal = impotency (psychological + structural); annulment under both (a) + (c); ₹13,500 lump-sum alimony agreed.
  • Principles: (i) Petition/notice need only material facts, not minute details; (ii) Curable epilepsy disclosure ≠ fraud (old law) — contrasted with prolapse concealment = fraud post-1976; (iii) Doctor’s notes + father’s non-examination adverse; (iv) Post-1976 “material fact” widens fraud (Malvankar pre-1976 narrow view obsolete).

(b) Mental (5(ii)) — see §4.2

  • No Sec 12(2) time bar for (a)/(b) — but delay affects credibility; medical evidence essential. Distinguish divorce 13(1)(iii) (post-marriage incurable).

(c) Force / Fraud — Babui Panmato + Asha Qureshi + P. v. K.

  • Text evolution (MUST quote): Pre-1976: “consent obtained by force or fraud” (narrow — fraud as to nature of ceremony only, e.g., Anath Nath De Cal). Post-1976: “fraud as to nature of ceremony OR as to material fact/circumstance concerning respondent” — wide; covers age, virginity/pregnancy, disease, impotency concealment, prior marriage/widowhood.
  • Babui Panmato Kuer v. Ram Agya Singh AIR 1968 Pat 190 (DU p.66, pre-1976 but liberally construed): 18+ girl overhears father tell mother groom 25–30, affluent; heavily veiled at May 1959 marriage; discovers 15-4-60 husband 60+ & poor; no cohabitation; files Mar 1961. Trial rejects (no direct representation + fraud must be at solemnisation). High Court reverses: Mother = agent; father’s suggestion he knew false + active concealment + duty to disclose (Contract Act Sec 17 + Ill.(b) horse-daughter) = fraud; Sec 12(1)(c) has no “at time of marriage” (unlike (a)(d)) → pre-marriage negotiation fraud suffices; Calcutta view rejected; annulment granted. Note: Som Dutt (P&H 1986) — 7-yr age gap + hysteria = gross matrimonial fraud.
  • Asha Qureshi v. Afaq Qureshi AIR 2002 MP 263 (DU p.33, SMA Sec 25(iii) — pari materia with HMA 12(1)(c)): SMA marriage 23-1-90; husband discovers wife earlier married to Motilal (dead), suppressed widowhood; believed virgin; wife claims disclosure but pleading vague, no date/time, contradictory (neighbours vs self). Held: Contract Act Sec 17 fraud = suggestion false / active concealment / act fitted to deceive; Explanation: silence = fraud where duty to speak; prior marriage/widowhood = material fact; duty to intimate; husband would not have married if known; Iqrarnama (Ex P/1) non-mention immaterial (not by legal expert); nullity under SMA 25(iii) affirmed. DU teaches as HMA fraud analogy.
  • Fraud checklist for answers: (1) Fact material? (age gap 30 yrs, prolapse, widowhood, pre-marriage child — yes; caste/job exaggeration — usually no), (2) Knowledge + intent to induce?, (3) Duty to speak? (fiduciary/family — yes), (4) Consent but for fraud? (would not have married), (5) Within 1 yr + no post-discovery cohabitation?

(d) Pre-marriage pregnancy by other — 2013 Q3, 2015 Q3 staple

  • Need all three 12(2)(b) bars satisfied. Husband ignorant + 1-yr + no consensual sex after discovery. DNA/child-adoption facts help. Alternative: fraud (c) if pregnancy concealed = material fact (overlap allowed — plead both).

CASE 01 • DU p.1 • PDF pp.8–11

Dr. Surajmani Stella Kujur v. Durga Charan Hansdah, AIR 2001 SC 938 : (2001) 3 SCC 13

Bench: R.P. Sethi, J. (SC) • Syllabus: Sec 2 Applicability + Sec 3(a) Custom + Sec 494 + Art 20
One-liner: STs outside HMA; custom must be pleaded/proved; custom cannot create criminal offence.
📖 FACTS (DU paras 6–7, 13–14): Appellant Oraon (tribal, Stella Kujur) + Respondent Santhal (Hansdah) — admittedly tribals, both tribes in Part XII, Constitution (ST) Order 1950 (as amended 1956/76/87/90). Appellant concedes: “two tribals who otherwise profess Hinduism, but marriage out of HMA purview per Sec 2(2), governed only by Santhal customs.” She complains to CMM New Delhi: marriage solemnized in Delhi “per Hindu rites”; respondent bigamously married Accused 2 without divorce → offence 494 IPC. Complaint nowhere pleads custom prohibiting second marriage or making it void. In court statement 24-10-92 she says “I am Hindu by religion.” Trial dismisses (“no pleading/evidence of custom; book alone insufficient”); High Court affirms. Appeal to SC. She now relies on alleged Santhal custom mandating monogamy.

❓ ISSUES: (1) Who is Hindu for HMA? Does HMA apply to STs professing Hinduism? (2) Can unpleaded/unproved custom make second marriage void to ground 494? (3) Can custom create an offence consistently with Art 20 + Sec 3(37) General Clauses Act?

⚔️ Arguments — Appellant (wife-complainant)
  • Parties profess Hinduism, married per Hindu rites → HMA applies; alternatively Santhal custom mandates monogamy → second marriage void → 494 attracted.
  • Absence of notification irrelevant; de facto Hindu way of life should govern; deny 494 = deny tribal women protection.
  • Book/text on Santhal custom + assertion suffices; strict proof should not defeat justice.
⚔️ Arguments — Respondent (husband) + State
  • Sec 2(2) expressly excludes STs unless Centre notifies — no notification; conceded tribals → HMA inapplicable, Sec 5/11/17 cannot be invoked.
  • No custom pleaded/proved: complaint silent; mere monogamy assertion ≠ voidness; need ancient/certain/reasonable + void-effect custom proved by clear evidence.
  • Custom deals with civil rights/status; cannot create penal offence (Art 20: “law in force”; Art 13(3): legislature-made law). 494 needs first + second valid solemnized marriages + voidness — none proved.

⚖️ DECISION (Appeal dismissed, DU paras 4–5, 8–10, 16–17): SC holds: (a) Sec 2(1) comprehensive (Hindu/Buddhist/Jaina/Sikh + domiciled non-Muslim/Christian/Parsi/Jew), but Sec 2(2) carves out STs; parties admitted STs → HMA inapplicable; even if notification under Art 342, further 2(2) notification needed. (b) “Hindu” undefined — Bhagwan Koer elastic test — but statutory exclusion prevails over profession. (c) Custom definition Sec 3(a) + Ramalakshmi Ammal strict proof: ancient, invariable, certain, reasonable, clear evidence; here neither pleaded (complaint + statement vague) nor proved; book reference insufficient. Mere monogamy plea ≠ plea that second marriage void/null/non-est — sine qua non for 494. (d) No custom-created offence: Art 20 + Sec 3(37) GCA: offence = act punishable by law; custom = civil rights source only (Sec 29 saves customary divorce/dissolution, not crimes). DU 2017 Q1(a) • 10 MARKS • Prohibited + 7-A Bigamy

1970 Gopal (Chennai) marries sister’s daughter Jaya per Hindu rites. 1980 marries Lakshmi by tying thirumangalam. Prosecuted 494. Defences: (i) no saptapadi with Lakshmi, (ii) M1 with Jaya hit by prohibited relationship. Discuss tenability.

Model Answer — 10 marks = 5 + 5
ISSUE 1 — Is M2 with Lakshmi validly solemnized without saptapadi? NO DEFENCE. CONVICTION LIKELY.
Rule: Sec 7 + 7-A TN + Sec 17 + 494 ingredients (Nagalingam). Saptapadi binds only where rites include it (Sec 7(2)); TN 7-A: declaration/garland/thali before relatives/friends = valid notwithstanding Sec 7.
Application: Gopal Chennai + M2 in TN → 7-A applies (Nagalingam identical: Thiruthani thirumangalam + garlands + kanniyathan). Tying thirumangalam = Sec 7-A(1)(c). No plea saptapadi essential in community (Nagalingam: essential only if admitted — Priya Bala line distinguished). If prosecution proves thali + witnesses/priest (PW3-like), M2 solemnized → void under Sec 17 (M1 subsisting) → 494 complete. Mere “no saptapadi” fails.
ISSUE 2 — Is M1 (maternal uncle–niece) void for prohibited degrees? DEPENDS ON CUSTOM. IF VOID, 494 FAILS.
Rule: Sec 3(g)(iv) uncle–niece = prohibited; Sec 5(iv) + 11 = void unless custom governing each permits. South mama–bhanji custom often permits (needs Surajmani strict proof). If M1 void ab initio, no subsisting spouse → M2 not bigamous (494 needs valid subsisting M1 — Lokhande/Pinninti logic).
Application: Sister’s daughter = niece squarely (iv). Gopal must plead + prove by clear evidence that Tamil custom permits uncle–niece (ancient/certain/reasonable). If proved → M1 valid → M2 bigamous (convict). If not proved → M1 void → prosecution collapses (acquit of 494; M2 validity then tested alone). Prosecution should first prove M1 validity + custom absence.
CONCLUSION: Defence (i) untenable per Nagalingam; defence (ii) arguable — outcome turns on custom proof. Advise prosecution to lead custom-negative + 7-A solemnisation evidence; advise Gopal to attack M1 validity (double-edged: destroys his own M1 legitimacy claims). Cases: Nagalingam, Lokhande, Surajmani (custom proof).
DU 2017 Q2(a) • 10 MARKS • Sec 12(a)(c) Prolapse (P. v. K. verbatim)

Rajendra–Seema marriage unconsummated due to Seema’s uterine prolapse. 2015 petition 12(1)(a)+(c). Seema: no defect before/after, no knowledge. Decide.

ISSUE: Annulment for impotency + fraud concealment?
RULE: Sec 12(1)(a) (impotent + non-consummation) + 12(1)(c) post-1976 (fraud as to material fact) + 12(2)(a) bars (1 yr from discovery + no post-discovery cohabitation). P. v. K. identical.
APPLICATION: Follow Mody J.: (1) Medical: 2nd-degree prolapse → intercourse only after manual manipulation + disgust → not normal intercourse → practical impossibility = impotency (structural + psychological resistance to conceal). Need Dr evidence (Bhatia-like) + notes. (2) Non-consummation: husband’s consistent account believed if wife “completely unreliable”; need to show repulse from night 1. (3) Fraud: prolapse concealment = material fact (distinguish curable epilepsy non-fraud); duty to disclose; Seema’s bare denial + parents’ silence disbelieved; father’s non-examination adverse. (4) Bars: petition within 1 yr of discovery (medical date) + no consensual cohabitation after → maintainable. Notice/petition need only material facts.
CONCLUSION: Rajendra likely succeeds under both (a) + (c) if medical + non-consummation proved; decree of nullity + lump-sum alimony (P. v. K. ₹13,500 analogy, now higher). If Seema proves no prolapse/knowledge + consummation → dismiss. Cite: P. v. K., Babui (duty), Asha (material).
DU 2017 Q2(b) + 2013 Q2(i) + 2014(2) Q2 • Child Marriage Classic

Ram 21 marries Lakshmi 16. Validity under HMA + PCMA 2006? (Variants: Anuj 20–Manju 17; Sumeet 20–Deepa 17 + Islam + Zoha; Sagar 17–Preeti 15 + Salim/Buksana)

ISSUE: Under-age bride → void/voidable/valid?
RULE: HMA Sec 5(iii) 21/18 + Sec 11/12 exhaustive + Sec 18(a) + Sec 13(2)(iv) + PCMA Sec 2(a)(b), 3 (voidable at child’s option, 2 yrs after majority), 12 (void if enticed/forced/trafficked), 14 (injunction), 4–6 (maintenance/legitimacy), 9–11 (punishment). Cases: Pinninti (FB) + Lajja (FB).
APPLICATION: (1) HMA: Valid, neither void (11) nor voidable (12) per Pinninti (overrules Saramma; directory “may”; 13(2)(iv) would be otiose; CMRA never voided; factum valet via Sec 4). Only Sec 18(a) (2 yrs + ₹1L) + bride’s 13(2)(iv) divorce if married <15 + repudiates 15–18 (here 16/17 → 13(2)(iv) not available unless <15 variant like Sagar–Preeti 15 → available). (2) PCMA: Child marriage → voidable at Lakshmi’s option only (Sec 3; via guardian/CMPO if minor; within 2 yrs of 18). Husband cannot invoke his own wrong. Void only if Sec 12/14 (enticement/force/sale/injunction) — not on bare age facts. If Lakshmi affirms/no petition → marriage stands valid. Maintenance/residence if annulled (Sec 4); son legitimate (Sec 6). (3) Conversion-bigamy variant (Anuj/Sumeet/Sagar): “M1 void” defence fails (Pinninti+Lajja); “now Muslim” fails (Lily Thomas: conversion doesn’t dissolve HMA; M2 void + 494); “M2 not Sec 7” — if M2 Nikah duly solemnized → 494; if Hindu garlands-only in Lucknow/Delhi → Lokhande may save (no solemnisation) — argue but Nikah likely proved.
CONCLUSION: Marriage valid under HMA, voidable at girl’s option under PCMA, punishable. Prosecute second marriage for 494; advise girl of Sec 3 PCMA + Sec 18/125/DV remedies. Cases: Pinninti, Lajja (+Tandon critique), Lily Thomas, Nagalingam/Lokhande for M2.
DU 2015 Q1 • 2014(2) Q1 • 2013 Q1 • 2012 Q1 • 2010 Q2 • Validity Problems (4–5 sub-parts)

Discuss validity under Hindu law: (i) paternal grandfather’s sister’s son’s son / brother’s daughter’s daughter variants, (ii) maternal uncle’s daughter / uncle-niece, (iii) stepson–widow / son’s wife / brother’s widow, (iv) garlands-only Chennai/Delhi, (v) 12F–20M / 16F–17M without consent.

METHOD (5 marks each sub-part): Test Sec 2 (two Hindus?) → Sec 5(i) → 5(iv) 3(g) → 5(v) 3(f) → Sec 7 → Sec 11/12/18. Always add “unless custom governing each permits (strict proof Surajmani).”

(i) Paternal grandfather’s sister’s son’s son / brother’s daughter’s daughter (2015/13/14(2)/12): VALID. Not lineal (i), not son’s-wife-type (ii), not brother/uncle’s-wife (iii), not sibling/uncle-niece/first-cousin (iv) — beyond 3(g) list. Sapinda: common ancestor = paternal great-great-grandfather or beyond → exceeds 5 through father for at least one → not sapinda. No 5(i) bar → valid + Sec 7 needed. Contrast if custom prohibits (rare).
(ii) Uncle–niece (2012) / maternal uncle’s daughter = cross-cousin (2015): UNCLE–NIECE VOID; COUSIN VOID (unless South custom). Uncle–niece = 3(g)(iv) → 5(iv) → void 11 + 18(b). Maternal uncle’s daughter = children of brother–sister = (iv) first cousins → void. South mama-bhanji / cross-cousin custom may save if proved (each party). Draw chain.
(iii) Stepson–widow / son’s divorced wife / elder brother’s pregnant widow (2015/13/14(2)): Son’s wife = VOID 3(g)(ii); Brother’s widow = VOID 3(g)(iii) unless karewa custom (Darbara Singh); Stepson–stepmother = VOID (ii) lineal-affinal. Pregnancy adds 12(1)(d) if husband ignorant, but voidness under 11 dominates. Muslim variants: brother’s widow in iddat = irregular till iddat; son’s wife = prohibited (void).
(iv) Delhi residents garland-only in Chennai temple (2012) / Lucknow rings+garlands (2014(2)): NO MARRIAGE (not solemnized). Sec 7 needs customary rites of either + saptapadi if included; mere garlands ≠ homa+saptapadi (Lokhande); Sec 7-A TN inapplicable (parties not TN / no thali/declaration per 7-A). No solemnisation → no marriage at all (a fortiori no 11/17/494). If custom of caste allows garland-only → must prove strictly.
(v) 12F–20M (2012) / 16F–17M without consent (2014(2)/2010): VALID under HMA (Pinninti), VOIDABLE at child’s option under PCMA (Lajja). No 11/12; Sec 18(a) + PCMA 9–11 punish; girl 13(2)(iv) if <15 + repudiates 15–18; consent of minor irrelevant to HMA validity (factum valet) but relevant to 363/376 + PCMA 12.
Closing line: Add Sec 16 legitimacy for any void/voidable + Seema registration presumption.
DU 2014 Q2(a) • 2013 Q2 • 2010 Q4 • Conversion-Bigamy Essay (10 marks)

Malti–Mohan (Hindu rites). Mohan fails to get 13B consent, converts with Vanita to Islam, Nikah. Malti files 494. Is Mohan guilty? (Variants: Anuj→Islam→Muslim girl; Sagar→Salim→Buksana; Sumeet→Zoha)

ISSUE: Does conversion + Nikah during subsisting HMA marriage = void M2 + 494?
RULE: Sec 5(i)+11+17 (bodily lifts 494/495) + 494 ingredients (Gopal Lal) + conversion law (Lily Thomas paras 30–32 + Sarla Mudgal) + CrPC 198 complaint + solemnisation proof (Lokhande/Nagalingam).
APPLICATION: (1) M1 valid HMA, subsisting (no death/divorce/decree). (2) Conversion does not dissolve M1 — only Sec 13(1)(ii)/10 ground; status + obligations continue. Feigned conversion (no belief, retains Hindu name/records like G.C. Ghosh) = fraud, no protection; even bona fide conversion same result till decree. (3) M2 Nikah during M1 = void (Sushmita Ghosh Q answered yes) — Hindu monogamy follows him; Islam polygamy cannot be invoked to defeat HMA. (4) 494: M1 valid + subsisting + M2 duly solemnized (Nikah: offer/acceptance/witnesses — must be proved like Sec 7 rites; if proved → convict 7 yrs, 10 yrs if concealed per 495). (5) Defences: “M1 child marriage void” → fails (Pinninti/Lajja); “now Muslim” → fails (Lily); “M2 not Sec 7” → irrelevant (M2 tested by Muslim solemnisation, not Sec 7). (6) Complaint by Malti maintainable (198(1)(c)). Art 20/25 review rejected in Lily.
CONCLUSION: Mohan guilty of bigamy (void M2 + 494). Advise Malti: 494 prosecution + divorce 13(1)(i)/(ii) + 125/DV maintenance + 13B needs free consent (no coercion). Advise Mohan: obtain divorce first; UCC obiter noted. Cases: Lily Thomas, Sarla Mudgal, Nagalingam (proof), Surajmani (tribal contrast).
DU 2014 Q2(b) • Bigamy Proof Theory (10 marks)

“For 494 conviction, second marriage must be validly performed.” Explain with two cases.

THESIS: “Whoever marries” = “whoever marries validly” (Lokhande). Voidness under Sec 17 is ingredient (Gopal Lal). Mere intent/cohabitation/admission insufficient (Kanwal Ram).
CASE 1 — Lokhande (acquittal): Garlands + forehead-touch, no homa/saptapadi, no custom proof → not solemnized → not void under 17 → no 494. Gandharva needs same essentials unless custom abrogation definitely proved. Strict proof protects liberty.
CASE 2 — Nagalingam (conviction): TN thali+garlands+kanniyathan = Sec 7-A valid notwithstanding Sec 7; no admission saptapadi essential → solemnized → void under 17 → 494. Shows “valid” = per law applicable to parties (Sec 7 or 7-A or custom).
SYNTHESIS + 3rd case: Add Priya Bala (agreed essentials → acquittal) + Lily (Nikah solemnisation for converted M2). Conclude: prosecution must prove M1 valid+subsisting + M2 solemnized per personal law; Sec 114 presumption cannot substitute in criminal trial. DU 2013 Q3 • 2015 Q3 • Fraud / Pregnancy / Impotency (10–20 marks)

Amit–Sunita: Amit discovers Sunita bore pre-marriage child (illicit, given in adoption). Remedies? (Variant: Bhushan impotent + mental concealment; Karuna bigamously marries Vineet — offences by all?)

ISSUES: Annulment 12(1)(c)/(d)/(a)? Bigamy 494? Fraud proof?
RULE: Sec 12(1)(c) post-1976 (material fact) + 12(2)(a) (1 yr + no post-discovery cohabitation) + 12(1)(d) (pregnant by other + 12(2)(b) triple bar) + 12(1)(a) (impotency) + 5(ii)/12(1)(b) (mental) + 494. Cases: Babui, Asha, P. v. K.
APPLICATION — Amit: Pre-marriage child = material fact (virginity/chastity + pregnancy history). If Sunita pregnant at marriage by other + Amit ignorant + petition within 1 yr of marriage + no consensual sex since discovery → 12(1)(d). Even if birth before marriage (not pregnant at marriage), concealment = 12(1)(c) fraud (Asha: duty to speak; Babui: active concealment). File within 1 yr of discovery; stop cohabitation after. Alternative divorce: cruelty 13(1)(ia) if condoned. No 494 by Amit.
VARIANT — Bhushan/Karuna/Vineet (2015 Q3): Bhushan impotent → Karuna has 12(1)(a) (if non-consummation) + 12(1)(c) (mental concealment if unfit per 5(ii)(b) + fraud). But Karuna cannot self-help marry Vineet while M1 subsists (voidable still valid till decree!) → Karuna–Vineet M2 = void 11 + 17 + 494 for Karuna (and Vineet abets 494/109 + 497 adultery then, now civil). Bhushan: no offence (impotency/mental not penal). Advise Karuna to first annul then remarry.
CONCLUSION: Amit → 12(1)(c)/(d) annulment likely; Karuna → prosecute for bigamy, Vineet abetment; Bhushan → annulment respondent, no crime.

DU Short Notes (2010 Q1 • 2014 Q1 • Expected) • 5 marks each

(a) Void vs Voidable (b) Sapinda (c) Child marriage HMA vs PCMA (d) Fraud (e) Registration (f) Conversion effect

(a) Void vs Voidable (2010c, 2014): Table §8.2: void = 5(i)(iv)(v)+11, ab initio, ipso jure, either party, no limit, declaratory; voidable = 12(a)–(d), valid till annulled, aggrieved only, 1-yr bars, constitutive; children legitimate both (Sec 16, limited to parents’ property). Cases: Pinninti (exhaustive), Lily (void), P. v. K./Babui (voidable).

(b) Sapinda (2010a): Sec 3(f): Fa 5 / Mo 3 inclusive, self=1; ascendant or common ascendant within limits each; includes half/uterine/illegitimate/adoptive; violation → void 11 + 18(b) unless each’s custom permits (strict proof). Example + counting diagram.

(c) Child marriage (2010e, 2014c): HMA: valid, neither void nor voidable (Pinninti), Sec 18(a) 2 yrs/₹1L + 13(2)(iv) girl <15 repudiates 15–18. PCMA: voidable at child’s option Sec 3 (2 yrs after majority) + void Sec 12/14 (enticed/forced/sold/injunction) + maintenance/legitimacy/punishment/CMPO (Lajja). Critique: HMA 11/12 unamended (Tandon).

(d) Fraud (2014(2) Q4b): Pre-1976 narrow (nature of ceremony) → post-1976 wide (material fact/circumstance). Contract 17 + duty to speak. Illustrations: age 60→25 (Babui), widowhood (Asha), prolapse (P. v. K.), pre-child (2013 Q3). Bars: 1 yr + no post-discovery cohabitation.

(e) Registration (Expected): Sec 8 proof-not-validity (8(5)); Seema: rebuttable presumption, all religions compulsory, age+status, 3-month rules, NCW 7 benefits; non-registration = fine + loss of presumption, not invalidity; certificate alone ≠ solemnisation proof for 494.

(f) Conversion (2010d): Hindu: conversion doesn’t dissolve (13/10 ground only), M2 void + 494 (Lily/Sarla); Muslim: apostasy effects differ (note: pre-1939 variant, now 1939 Act). Feigned vs bona fide irrelevant to subsistence.
