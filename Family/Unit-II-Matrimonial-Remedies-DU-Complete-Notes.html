1.1 From Sacrament to Breakdown — Evolution (Lexis p.29)

TheoryCore ideaHMA embodimentCritique
1. Fault / Matrimonial-offence theoryMarriage broken by guilt of one party; innocent petitions. Adversarial, mud-slinging.Sec 13(1)(i)–(ib),(ii)–(vii) + 13(2); Sec 23 bars (condonation, collusion, connivance). Original 1955 scheme (Derrett: “for helpless ill-treated women, not husbands’ variety”).Time-consuming, expensive, embittering; blocks ancillary settlement; no exit where no fault but marriage dead shell.

Consent theoryMarriage = partnership dissoluble by mutual will + safeguards.Sec 13B (1976) (SMA Sec 28 model 1954): 1-yr separate + joint petition + 6–18 mo motion + inquiry (genuineness, no force/fraud/undue influence Sec 23(1)(bb)).Fails where recalcitrant spouse withholds consent vindictively; needs breakdown supplement.
3. Breakdown theoryLaw notices fact of death, not fault; separation + no reunion = divorce. Exam line (Samar Ghosh, DU p.136–137, quoting 71st Report + Lodder v. Lodder 1921 NZLR 786, Salmond J.): “When matrimonial relation has for [years] ceased de facto, it should cease de jure also… essential purposes frustrated; continuance not merely useless but mischievous… marriage merely a shell out of which substance gone… divorce as solution/escape route, unconcerned with past wrongs.” + Naveen Kohli v. Neelu Kohli (2006) 4 SCC 558, paras 74–76: long separation → bond beyond repair → fiction supported by legal tie → refusing severance shows scant regard; public interest lies in recognising wreck.

1.2 71st Law Commission Report (7 April 1978) — “Irretrievable Breakdown as Ground”

  • Context (Samar Ghosh extract, DU pp.135–137): 20+ yrs debate (now 50): fault vs breakdown; Commonwealth germ: NZ 1920 Act (3-yr separation agreement → discretionary divorce).
  • Recommendation: Add breakdown ground: 3 yrs continuous separation (3-month reconciliation tolerance) → presumption of breakdown; court must attempt reconciliation first; if irreparable → grant.
  • Safeguards proposed: (i) financial protection of wife/children (maintenance + property), (ii) no divorce causing grave hardship, (iii) children’s welfare paramount, (iv) court discretion, not automatic.
  • Majority vs minority: Majority (most jurists): life short, misery cannot continue indefinitely; law must respond. Minority: doors to litigation, more problems than solved (Samar para 77–78).
  • Aftermath: 1981 Bill (IRBM) lapsed (women’s groups’ opposition); 217th Report (2009) revived; SC in Naveen Kohli, Samar Ghosh, Rishikesh Sharma (2006) 12 SCALE 282, Chetan Dass (2001) 4 SCC 250 repeatedly recommended Parliament to enact.

Sec 9 — Restitution of Conjugal RightsSYLLABUS ITEM 1 • WORKING-WIFE PROBLEM = 7/8 PAPERS • KAILASHWATI (74) • SWARAJ GARG (85) • SAROJ RANI (93)

“When either spouse has, without reasonable excuse, withdrawn from the society of the other, the aggrieved party may apply… court, on satisfaction of truth + no legal bar, shall decree restitution.” (Sec 9). Mildest remedy — compels cohabitation, not divorce; but feeds Sec 13(1A)(ii) after 1 yr.

2.1 Provision Text + Ingredients (Sec 9 + Explanation, 1976)

HMA Sec 9 — RCR “Where either the husband or the wife has, without reasonable excuse, withdrawn from the society of the other, the aggrieved party may apply… and the court, on being satisfied of the truth of the statements… and there is no legal ground why the application should not be granted, may decree restitution of conjugal rights accordingly. Explanation (1976): where question arises whether there has been reasonable excuse… burden of proving reasonable excuse shall be on the person who has withdrawn from society.” Bellwether phrase DU expects: “withdrawn + without reasonable excuse + burden on withdrawer”.
IngredientMeaning + DU point
1. Valid marriage subsistingOnly spouses can sue; void marriage = no RCR (only Sec 11 declaration).
2. Withdrawal from societyRefusal to cohabit/co-reside; need not be physical desertion — living apart + refusal to return suffices; animus to stay away (compare desertion elements).
3. Without reasonable excuseOpen-textured: cruelty, impotence, husband’s cruelty/neglect, wife’s genuine employment? (Kailashwati NO vs Swaraj YES — the DU debate); Muslim second marriage = excuse for first wife (PYQ 2011 Q3 — answer: yes, Itwari v. Asghari + Sec 3 MWA analogy; Hindu second marriage void anyway = excuse/cruelty).
4. No legal barSec 23 filters apply (condonation of cruelty pleaded as excuse, collusion, delay).
5. Burden (Expl.)Withdrawer proves excuse; petitioner proves withdrawal. Court must attempt reconciliation (Sec 23(2)).
Effect of decree: directs resumption of cohabitation; no divorce; coercive enforcement limited — O21 R32 CPC: attachment of property only, NOT arrest (Harvinder Kaur affirmed in Saroj Rani: remedy is inducement/financial sanction, not forced cohabitation). Non-compliance 1 yr → Sec 13(1A)(ii) divorce for either party. Wife complying may still claim maintenance; RCR decree in husband’s favour can defeat wife’s Sec 125 CrPC claim (opposite if in wife’s favour — Pran/Radha logic).

2.2 Case 01 — Kailashwati v. Ayodhya Prakash (DU p.74) — “Working-wife RCR: employment is NO excuse” ⭐⭐⭐

Facts (DU pp.74–85 condensed) Marriage 12-5-1973 (Arya Samaj); lived Delhi (husband’s house + parents, 1st floor vacant portion available); wife nursing Superintendent at Irwin Hospital, also received Nightingale Award mid-case + foreign trip; marriage not consummated (wife slept separately, said “won’t permit sex on medical grounds”); wife left 7-7-1974 after demanding husband live separately from parents, refused return. Husband: RCR petition. Wife’s excuses: (i) no separate accommodation, (ii) demand of dowry/scooter, (iii) husband unemployed/financially dependent, no status, (iv) incompatible lifestyle.
Held (decree GRANTED to husband): (1) Wife’s gainful employment at distant place = NO reasonable excuse — she may work anywhere but cannot refuse to live with husband on that ground; husband offered vacant first floor; (2) Hindu wife’s duty (as then viewed): “live with husband, submit to his guardianship, protect/virtue”; separate-residence demand to isolate from parents = unreasonable; (3) unconsummated marriage + denial of sex = supports husband; (4) dowry/unemployment pleas unproved (no witness; husband educated, family property); (5) RCR is personal-law duty, not violative of equality — wife’s appeal dismissed.
How to cite (problem answers): “As in Kailashwati, mere employment / transfer / career preference does not constitute reasonable excuse where husband provides suitable matrimonial home; wife must prove grave cause (cruelty/neglect/unsuitability).” Contrast immediately with Swaraj Garg for balance marks.

2.3 Case 02 — Swaraj Garg v. K.M. Garg (DU p.85) — “Working-wife RCR: bona-fide job CAN be excuse” ⭐⭐⭐

Facts (DU pp.85–93 condensed) Marriage 22-11-1973; husband lecturer Delhi; wife teacher in Bhiwani (posted away); wife demanded transfer to Delhi (applied through proper channel, supported by husband initially); husband demanded resignation + return; wife refused to quit bona-fide service; husband: RCR. Trial court granted RCR; High Court reversed; Division Bench affirmed reversal.
Held (RCR REFUSED — wife’s excuse reasonable): (1) Wife in bona-fide government service before/after marriage, sincerely seeking transfer to Delhi — refusal to abandon career is reasonable excuse; (2) Modern working-wife equality: Kailashwati view of “wife must follow husband” outdated; marriage = partnership; (3) Husband knew of job at marriage — estopped from demanding resignation; (4) No cruelty/neglect by husband needed — excuse judged on wife’s bona fides; (5) Courts must balance: permanent-distinct career + genuine transfer efforts = reasonable; rejected blanket Kailashwati rule.
Exam move: Reconcile Kailashwati vs Swaraj (write this table for full 8 case-marks)Kailashwati (RCR granted)Swaraj Garg (RCR refused)
Wife’s jobDelhi itself; no transfer issue — job was pretext alongside non-consummation + separate-home demandBhiwani posting; transfer impossible immediately; sincere Delhi-transfer applications
Husband’s conductProvided home, willing to cohabit, no cruelty provedKnew of service at marriage; demanded resignation (unreasonable)
Ratio meldSingle test: Is the career plea bona fide + unavoidable, or a pretext for withholding society? Bona-fide distant service + genuine transfer efforts = reasonable excuse (Swaraj); local job + collateral demands (separate home, no sex) = no excuse (Kailashwati). Cite both in every working-wife PYQ.

2.4 Case 03 — Saroj Rani v. Sudarshan Kumar Chadha AIR 1984 SC 1562 (DU p.93) — “Sec 9 constitutionally VALID” ⭐⭐⭐

Journey to SC T. Sareetha v. T. Venkatasubbaiah AIR 1983 AP 356 (Choudary J.): Sec 9 struck down — violates Art 14 (husband-biased), Art 21 (privacy, sexual autonomy, bodily integrity); RCR = “savage and barbarous” coercion of unwilling wife into sex; Victorian relic (English law abolished RCR 1970). → Delhi HC Harvinder Kaur v. Harmander Singh AIR 1984 Del 66 (Rohatgi J.): upheld — RCR aims at cohabitation/consortium, not sexual intercourse; enforcement only attachment of property (O21 R32), no arrest; serves reconciliation/social purpose; Art 21 argument overblown. → SC (Saroj Rani) affirmed Harvinder Kaur.
SC ratio (short judgment, often quoted): (1) Sec 9 valid — serves social purpose: prevent hasty dissolution, foster reconciliation; (2) Decree = “inducement for husband/wife to live together”, not state-compelled sex — coercion limited to financial sanction; (3) Agrees with Harvinder Kaur’s aims + method + remedy reasoning; disapproves T. Sareetha; (4) Hindu marriage = sacrament + consortium, not mere contract; (5) Obiter: constitutional challenge to personal-law provisions needs circumspection (later revisited in Shayara Bano / Puttaswamy era — but Saroj Rani still good law, EXAM answer).
Attack + defend (both-side arguments for “validity of Sec 9” essay): Attack (T. Sareetha): Art 21 privacy/bodily autonomy (Puttaswamy 2017 strengthens: sexual autonomy fundamental), Art 14 (statistically anti-wife), Art 15(1) sex classification, modern gender justice. Defend (Harvinder/Saroj): gender-neutral text, reconciliation aim, only property attachment, Art 15(3)-adjacent family protection, 13(1A) safety valve after 1 yr. Conclude: binding SC validity; Puttaswamy cited by critics but no overruling — mention 2022–24 challenges pending (write “as of syllabus, Saroj Rani governs”).

Sec 10 Judicial Separation + Sec 13(1A) BridgeSYLLABUS “10 AND 13(1A)” • LEXIS pp.42–43 + HIRACHAND + MANISHA TYAGI

JS = “suspended animation” (Derrett): cohabitation suspended, marriage subsists, remarriage prohibited (Sec 15–17 punish), rescindable on reconciliation. Pre-1976 it was also the only relief for cruelty/desertion (“half-way house”); post-1976 full divorce available, but JS survives as softer option + Sec 13A alternate.

3.1 Provision Text (Sec 10, amended 1976)

HMA Sec 10 — Judicial Separation “(1) Either party to a marriage… may present a petition praying for a decree for judicial separation on any of the grounds specified in sub-section (1) of section 13, and in the case of a wife also on any of the grounds specified in sub-section (2) thereof, as grounds on which a petition for divorce might have been presented. (2) Where a decree for judicial separation has been passed it shall no longer be obligatory for the petitioner to cohabit with the respondent, but the court may, on application by petition of either party and on being satisfied of the truth of the statements… rescind the decree if it considers it just and reasonable to do so.”
Effect / FeatureLaw + DU cite
Status: married but separatedNo duty to cohabit (Sec 10(2)); no remarriage (marriage subsists — adultery during JS still matrimonial offence; Hirachand: tie subsists, only duty suspended). Maintenance/custody continue.
Grounds = same as divorcePost-1976: any 13(1)/13(2) ground; before 1976: cruelty/desertion gave only JS (“half-way house”), divorce needed 13(1A) 2-yr route — Lexis p.42; pre-1976 six JS grounds listed (adultery, cruelty, desertion, conversion, unsoundness, leprosy/VD).
Rescission on reconciliationEither party may apply; court rescinds if just/reasonable — embodiment of reconciliation policy (Derrett’s “reconsider / single-taste” purpose: taste separation before final divorce).
Bridge to divorce: Sec 13(1A)(i)1 yr after JS decree + no resumption of cohabitation → either party may seek divorce (even the guilty one — breakdown seeded); subject to Sec 23(1)(a) wrong (Dharmendra/Hirachand/Srinivasan).
Sec 13A alternate reliefIn any divorce petition (except 13B), if divorce not warranted, court may grant JS instead — see Sec 4.5.
Manisha Tyagi v. Deepak Kumar (2010) 4 SCC 339 (Lexis p.43): SC restored Single Judge’s JS decree (set aside DB’s suo-motu divorce): where evidence supports cruelty but marriage may survive / petitioner only sought JS, appellate courts must not convert to divorce suo motu. Exam use: JS vs divorce = distinct reliefs; respect petitioner’s choice; JS preserves reconciliation window.
Derrett’s prophecy (Lexis p.42): “[JS] will gradually wither away” once full divorce freely available — but DU examiner loves it: JS survives for (i) religious objectors to divorce, (ii) Sec 13A soft landing, (iii) property/maintenance strategy, (iv) cooling period before 13(1A).

4.3 Sec 13(1A) — Breakdown-Seeded Divorce (either party)

HMA Sec 13(1A) (1964; 1-yr since 1976) “Either party to a marriage, whether solemnised before or after commencement… may also present a petition for dissolution by decree of divorce on the ground — (i) that there has been no resumption of cohabitation as between the parties for one year or upwards after passing of a decree for judicial separation; or (ii) that there has been no restitution of conjugal rights as between the parties for one year or upwards after passing of a decree for restitution in a proceeding to which they were parties.”
Why revolutionary (DU essay point): remedy available to guilty/judgment-debtor spouse too — first legislative recognition that dead marriage should end regardless of fault. Limit: Sec 23(1)(a) “taking advantage of own wrong” — the Dharmendra–Hirachand–Srinivasan triangle (Full Sec 8).

4.4 Sec 14 — One-Year Bar + Sec 23/23A Filters

FilterRule
Sec 14: no petition within 1 yr of marriageException: exceptional hardship to petitioner or exceptional depravity of respondent (leave of court; court considers children + reconciliation chance + reasonable probability of reconciliation). Leave may be revoked if obtained by misrepresentation. 13B has its own 1-yr separation clock instead.
Sec 23(1)(a): own wrong/disabilityNo relief if petitioner takes advantage of own wrong/disability; must not have condoned, connived, colluded; no unnecessary/improper delay; (bb) 13B genuineness; (c) no collusion; (d) no condonation; (e) no other legal bar. Reconciliation duty: Sec 23(2). Dharmendra (mere non-compliance ≠ wrong) vs Hirachand/Srinivasan (positive misconduct = wrong).
Sec 23(1)(b) condonationDastane (DU pp.115–118): condonation = forgiveness + reinstatement with knowledge of offence; conditional on future good behaviour; revived by fresh cruelty; burden on respondent pleading it.
Sec 13A alternate reliefIn any divorce petition (not 13B), court may grant JS instead if just — soft landing; respects reconciliation policy.
Sec 23A respondent reliefRespondent may oppose on petitioner’s adultery/cruelty/desertion AND counter-claim any relief (divorce/JS/RCR) on that ground — no separate petition needed.
Standing exam trap: “Husband guilty of adultery seeks 13(1A) divorce — grant?” Answer: YES if only non-compliance (Dharmendra); NO if continuing adultery/maintenance default/thwarting reunion (Hirachand) — distinguish on positive misconduct. Always pair the two.

Sec 13(1)(ia) Cruelty — Physical + MentalDASTANE (100) • SAMAR GHOSH (120) • SANJEEV GUPTA 2019 ALL (SUPP.) • PYQ 2014 Q3 • 2015 Q4 • 2011Supp Q4

5.1 Case 04 — Dastane v. Dastane AIR 1975 SC 1534 (DU p.100) — “Grave & weighty; ordinary wear” ⭐⭐⭐

Facts (DU pp.100–123) Marriage 1956; wife (schizophrenia treatment before marriage — disclosed?) showed cruelty: abused husband’s father (“burn his degree”), threatened suicide/burn house, tore mangalsutra, taunted day/night, beat daughter Shubha, ill-treated servants; husband sought JS (pre-1976). Trial: granted; HC: reversed (condonation — parties cohabited till 1961 + letters). SC appeal.
SC ratio (Chandrachud J. — quote verbatim): (1) Definition: cruelty = conduct of “grave and weighty” nature — must be more serious than “ordinary wear and tear of married life”; (2) Apprehension test: petitioner must show conduct gives reasonable apprehension that living together will be harmful or injurious; (3) Intention irrelevant: mental illness/absence of malice is no defence if conduct is cruel in effect (wife’s schizophrenia plea rejected); (4) Condonation (Sec 23(1)(b), DU pp.115–118): = forgiveness + reinstatement with knowledge; conditional, revived by fresh cruelty; mere cohabitation/letters ≠ condonation where cruelty continued; burden on respondent; (5) Standard of proof: preponderance of probabilities (matrimonial, not criminal BRD) — even for adultery-adjacent facts; (6) Result: cruelty proved → JS restored (pre-1976); threats/insults/child-beating = grave-weighty.

Sec 13(1)(ib) Desertion — 2 Years ContinuousCASE 07 BIPINCHANDRA (141) • PYQ 2010 Q5 • 2013 Q8 • 2014 Q4 • 2014(2) Q5

Desertion = factum (separation) + animus deserendi (intent to forsake permanently) on desertor’s side, + absence of consent + absence of reasonable-cause conduct on deserted side — all four for the whole 2-yr period immediately before petition. Pre-1976: JS only; post-1976: full divorce.

6.1 Case 07 — Bipinchandra v. Prabhavati AIR 1957 SC 176 (DU p.141) — “4-element test; locus paenitentiae” ⭐⭐⭐

Facts (Bom HC Act Sec 3(d) — pari materia; DU pp.139–141, 148–157) Marriage 1942; wife left matrimonial home after disputes (alleged cruelty by husband’s family); husband’s solicitor letter 15-7-1947 + wife’s letter Ex.E showed husband unwilling to take her back / imposed conditions; husband sued desertion. Trial: dismissed; HC: granted; SC appeal.
SC ratio (dismissed — desertion NOT proved): (1) 4 elements (desertor: factum of separation + animus deserendi; deserted: no consent + no conduct giving reasonable cause); petitioner proves all; (2) Animus + factum must co-exist — separation without intent (job, illness, compulsion) ≠ desertion; intent inferred from conduct/letters; (3) Locus paenitentiae: bona-fide offer to return before suit ends desertion (repentance window); conditional/unwilling letters (Ex.E, 15-7-1947) showed husband’s unwillingness → he failed; (4) Constructive desertion (Lang v. Lang): spouse compelled to leave by other’s cruelty/neglect = the cruel spouse is the desertor; (5) Continuity: must persist whole statutory period (Pratt v. Pratt; Halsbury) — casual visits without resumption don’t break, but bona-fide reunion does; (6) Desertion is continuing offence — terminates only by resumption or supervening act.
Desertion sub-doctrineOne-line rule + cite
Animus inferenceFrom letters, refusal to return, remarriage attempt, settling elsewhere; mere living apart (service/education) insufficient.
ConsentAgreement to live apart (mutual, job posting) = consent → no desertion; consent revocable on bona-fide demand to return.
Reasonable causeCruelty, impotence, neglect, second marriage, unsuitable home = cause; then leaver is not desertor (constructive desertion flips).
2-yr computationContinuous immediately preceding petition; periods cannot be tacked across genuine resumption.
BurdenPetitioner proves separation + animus + non-consent; respondent pleading cause proves it (Sec 9 Expl. analogy).
Desertion vs RCR-withdrawal (exam distinction): RCR needs only “withdrawal without reasonable excuse” (no animus, no 2-yr period); desertion needs animus + 2 yrs + 4 elements. Every desertion includes withdrawal, but not vice versa. Failed desertion petition may still sustain RCR.

Sec 13(1A) + Sec 23(1)(a) “Wrong” — The Decisive TriangleCASE 08 DHARMENDRA (158) • CASE 09 SRINIVASAN (161) • CASE 10 HIRACHAND (168) • PYQ 2011 Q4b • 2012 Q5 • 2013 Q5a • 2014 Q5a

13(1A) lets even the guilty spouse seek divorce after 1-yr non-compliance — unless barred by Sec 23(1)(a): “taking advantage of his or her own wrong or disability.” Three DU cases draw the line: mere disinclination ≠ wrong (Dharmendra) vs positive misconduct = wrong (Hirachand, Srinivasan).

8.1 Case 08 — Dharmendra Kumar v. Usha Kumar AIR 1977 SC 2213 (DU p.158) — “Mere non-compliance is NOT wrong” ⭐⭐⭐

Facts (DU pp.158–161) Wife obtained RCR decree; husband did not comply; after statutory period (then 2 yrs) husband (judgment-debtor, “guilty” party) petitioned u/Sec 13(1A)(ii). Wife: barred by Sec 23(1)(a) — he takes advantage of own disobedience. Trial/HC: denied. SC appeal.
SC ratio (GRANTED divorce to guilty husband): (1) Sec 13(1A) expressly open to either party — Parliament intended guilty spouse to benefit (breakdown policy); (2) Mere disinclination / failure to comply with RCR decree is NOT “wrong” u/Sec 23(1)(a) — wrong needs grave misconduct / positive act beyond non-compliance; (3) Approved Ram Kali v. Gopal Dass, Gajna Devi v. Purshotam (P&H) taking same view; (4) To hold otherwise = render “either party” otiose; (5) Reconciliation policy yields to reality of dead marriage after 1 yr.

8.2 Case 10 — Hirachand Srinivas Managaonkar v. Sunanda (2001) 4 SCC 125 (DU p.168) — “Continuing adultery + maintenance default = wrong” ⭐⭐⭐

Facts (DU pp.152–175) Wife obtained JS on adultery; husband ordered to pay Rs.100 maintenance; husband continued cohabiting with mistress + wilfully defaulted maintenance (“marking time” till 13(1A) clock ran); then sought divorce u/Sec 13(1A)(i). Trial: granted; HC: reversed; SC appeal.
SC ratio (DENIED divorce — wrong bar applies): (1) Adultery is a continuing offence — Sec 10(2) only suspends duty to cohabit; marital tie subsists; post-JS mistress-cohabitation = fresh/continuing wrong; (2) Wilful maintenance default while awaiting limitation = exploiting own wrong (“marking time”); (3) Distinguished Dharmendra: there mere non-compliance; here positive misconduct; (4) Disapproved Sumitra Manna, Bai Mani (lenient views); approved Soundarammal; (5) Sec 23(1)(a) is ethical filter — court must examine petitioner’s post-decree conduct.

8.3 Case 09 — T. Srinivasan v. T. Varalakshmi, Madras HC + affirmed AIR 1999 SC 595 (DU p.161) — “RCR decree as weapon + driving wife away = wrong” ⭐⭐⭐

Facts (DU pp.161–168) Husband obtained ex-parte RCR decree (to thwart wife’s reunion efforts); on 23-5-1977 drove wife away from home; wife gave police writing/complaint; husband sent notices Ex.A-4/5/6/8 demanding return (paper trail for 13(1A)); then sought divorce u/Sec 13(1A)(ii). Trial: granted; Madras HC: reversed; SC: affirmed HC.
Ratio (DENIED — affirmed AIR 1999 SC 595): (1) Obtaining RCR decree as a device to thwart reunion + physically driving wife away + police complaint = positive wrong/misconduct, not mere disinclination; (2) Notices Ex.A-4/5/6/8 = manufactured evidence, belied by conduct; (3) Follows Hirachand distinction; Dharmendra inapplicable where petitioner engineered non-compliance; (4) Equity: “he who seeks equity must do equity” — Sec 23(1)(a) bars engineered breakdown.

Sec 13B Mutual Consent Divorce — Consent, Cooling, Withdrawal, WaiverCASE 11 SURESTA (174) • CASE 12 AMARDEEP 2017 (SUPP.) • HITESH • ANIL JAIN • SHILPA SAILESH 2023 • PYQ 2011 Q4a • 2013 Q5b • 2014 Q5b • 2017 Q5

Inserted 1976 (SMA Sec 28 model): divorce by joint will + double motion + inquiry. Three litigation triggers: (i) withdrawal of consent before decree (Sureshta — allowed), (ii) waiver of 6-month cooling (Amardeep — directory), (iii) fraud/undue influence (Sec 23(1)(bb)).

9.1 Provision Text (Sec 13B + Sec 23(1)(bb))

HMA Sec 13B — Divorce by Mutual Consent “(1) …a petition for dissolution of marriage by a decree of divorce may be presented to the district court by both the parties… on the ground that they have been living separately for one year or more, that they have not been able to live together and that they have mutually agreed that the marriage should be dissolved. (2) On the motion of both the parties made not earlier than six months after the date of presentation… and not later than eighteen months… if the petition is not withdrawn in the meantime, the court shall, on being satisfied, after hearing the parties and after making such inquiry as it thinks fit, that a marriage has been solemnised and that the averments in the petition are true, pass a decree of divorce declaring the marriage to be dissolved with effect from the date of the decree.”
Sec 23(1)(bb) (1976): in 13B cases, court must be satisfied consent was not obtained by force, fraud or undue influence.
RequirementMeaning (Sureshta + later)
1. Living separately ≥1 yr immediately before petitionNot living as husband and wife — place irrelevant (same roof + no marital relations = separate); no desire to perform marital obligations; continuous year.
2. Unable to live togetherMarriage broken down; mere living apart for job ≠ unable; court tests genuineness at inquiry.
3. Mutual agreementFree, subsisting consensus; vitiated by force/fraud/undue influence (Sec 23(1)(bb)) — e.g., property extortion then refusal (PYQ 2011 Q4a Maya).
4. Joint motion 6–18 monthsBoth parties move together (second motion); gap = interregnum for second thought/reconciliation; 6-mo floor waivable (Amardeep), 18-mo ceiling strict-ish.
5. Inquiry + no withdrawalCourt hears parties, verifies averments; petition not withdrawn in meantime — consent must subsist till decree.

9.2 Case 11 — Sureshta Devi v. Om Prakash AIR 1991 SC 190 (DU p.174) — “Consent withdrawable till decree” ⭐⭐⭐

Facts (DU pp.174–185) Joint 13B petition filed; before second motion wife withdrew consent (alleged pressure + property terms unfulfilled); husband pressed decree on first petition. HC conflict: Bombay/Delhi/MP views allowed decree despite withdrawal. SC reference.
SC ratio (consent must SUBSIST): (1) Three substantive requirements u/Sec 13B(1) (1-yr separate + unable + mutual agreement) must exist at filing AND continue; (2) Words “if the petition is not withdrawn in the meantime” = either party may unilaterally withdraw anytime before decree; (3) 6–18 mo gap is cooling/second-thought period — withdrawing is its very purpose; (4) O23 R1 CPC analogy rejected (not ordinary suit withdrawal); consent at second motion must be free + subsisting; (5) Overruled Bombay/Delhi/MP views permitting one-sided decree; (6) Result: no decree without wife’s continuing consent — petition fails.
PYQ 2011 Q4a (Maya/Anil) answer: “Per Sureshta, Anil cannot get decree on first petition alone — Maya’s refusal at second motion = withdrawal; court must dismiss 13B petition. Remedy: sue separately u/Sec 13(1) if cruelty/desertion proved, or recover property under general law (fraud/contract).” Also add Sec 23(1)(bb): property-extorted consent = voidable.

9.3 Case 12 — Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur (2017) 8 SCC 746 — “6-month cooling DIRECTORY; Family Court can waive” ⭐⭐⭐ (supplementary: Khaitan uploads + SCC)

Facts Marriage broken; 8+ yrs separation; joint 13B petition; parties sought waiver of 6-month period u/Sec 13B(2) (settlement complete, mediation failed). Question: mandatory or directory? Who can waive — only SC under Art 142 (Manish Goel) or Family Court itself?
SC ratio (waiver possible — guided discretion): (1) Sec 13B(2) 6-month period is directory, not mandatory (Kailash v. Nanhku principle); (2) Family Court itself can waive — no need for Art 142 transfer to SC (Manish Goel (2010) 4 SCC 393 binding only on Art-142 point, distinguished); (3) Four waiver conditions (ALL needed): (a) Sec 13B(1) 1-yr separation complete + 18 months total before first motion; (b) mediation/conciliation failed (O32A R3 CPC, Sec 23(2) HMA, Sec 9 FCA — record efforts); (c) all ancillary settled (alimony, custody, property); (d) waiting would only prolong agony; (4) Discretion, not automatic — refuse if short separation / incomplete settlement / coercion / open custody; (5) Procedure: joint waiver application + settlement + separation affidavits + mediation record.

FactsMarried 1956; wife: abused FIL, suicide/burn threats, tore mangalsutra, taunts, beat daughter Shubha; schizophrenia-treatment history. Husband: JS (pre-1976). Trial granted; HC reversed (condonation); SC appeal.
  • Issues: (1) What is cruelty? (2) Intention/illness defence? (3) Condonation? (4) Standard of proof?
  • Husband: sustained grave acts; apprehension of harm; cohabitation ≠ forgiveness (cruelty continued).
  • Wife: ordinary quarrels; illness negates intention; condoned by cohabitation/letters till 1961.
  • Decision: JS RESTORED — cruelty proved; condonation not made out (revived).
Ratio: “grave and weighty” beyond ordinary wear; reasonable apprehension; intention irrelevant; condonation = forgiveness + reinstatement (conditional, revivable); preponderance standard. Exam: every cruelty answer’s foundation + condonation (23(1)(b)) PYQs.
Case 05 — Sanjeev Gupta v. Ritu Gupta, 2019 SCC OnLine All 2255 (supp.) — forced unnatural sex = cruelty
FactsMarried 1-7-2012 (Arya Samaj + Registrar); wedding-night forced sodomy/oral → bleeding; threats to 5-yr-old Khushi; 17-7-2012 compromise then repeat (incl. kidney-stone rest period); ADJ Ghaziabad granted divorce (29-3-2018); husband appealed (no cross on sodomy, no evidence).
  • Issues: (1) Forced unnatural sex = cruelty 13(1)(ia) + 13(2)(ii)? (2) Marital consent scope? (3) Standard of proof?
  • Wife: perverse acts without consent; injuries; continuing; privacy violation (Grace Jayamani, Bini John).
  • Husband: denial; matrimonial consent implied; criminal standard needed.
  • Decision: Divorce AFFIRMED — forced unnatural sex = cruelty + sodomy ground; forcible sex = privacy intrusion; preponderance; adverse inference.
Ratio: marriage ≠ consent to perverse acts; wife may plead 13(1)(ia) + 13(2)(ii) together. Exam: sexual-cruelty problems + sodomy ground.
Case 06 — Samar Ghosh v. Jaya Ghosh, (2007) 4 SCC 511 (DU p.120) — mental cruelty illustrations
FactsMarried 13-12-1984 (SMA); IAS officers; wife refused postings/cohabitation/child; separate since 27-8-1990; family court granted divorce; HC reversed; SC after 16.5 yrs separation (husband post-bypass, “no feeling”).
  • Issues: (1) Define mental cruelty? (2) Long separation = cruelty/IRBM? (3) Restore divorce?
  • Husband: unilateral decisions, deprivation of consortium, dead marriage (71st Report, Naveen Kohli).
  • Wife: career autonomy; no grave acts; IRBM not a ground.
  • Decision: Divorce RESTORED — 14 illustrations laid; long separation + deadness = cruelty (xiv).
Ratio: no straitjacket definition; 14 guidelines; forcing dead marriage = cruelty. Exam: quote 2–3 illustrations per problem + IRBM essays (71st Report extract).
Case 07 — Bipinchandra v. Prabhavati, AIR 1957 SC 176 (DU p.141) — desertion 4 elements
FactsMarried 1942; wife left after disputes; husband’s letters (Ex.E, 15-7-1947 solicitor) showed his unwillingness/conditions; husband sued desertion. Trial dismissed; HC granted; SC appeal.
  • Issues: (1) Elements of desertion? (2) Who is desertor? (3) Locus paenitentiae? (4) Continuity?
  • Husband: wife left + stayed away 2+ yrs; animus inferred.
  • Wife: compelled to leave (constructive); husband unwilling to receive (letters); no animus.
  • Decision: Appeal DISMISSED — petitioner failed (own unwillingness); wife not desertor.
Ratio: factum + animus + no consent + no reasonable cause; co-existence; locus paenitentiae; constructive desertion (Lang); whole-period continuity (Pratt). Exam: every desertion problem’s skeleton.
Case 08 — Dharmendra Kumar v. Usha Kumar, AIR 1977 SC 2213 (DU p.158) — guilty spouse CAN use 13(1A)
FactsWife held RCR decree; husband never complied; after limitation husband (judgment-debtor) sought Sec 13(1A)(ii) divorce. Wife: Sec 23(1)(a) bar. Courts below denied.
  • Issues: (1) “Either party” includes guilty spouse? (2) Is non-compliance “wrong”?
  • Husband: statute says either party; marriage dead; Ram Kali, Gajna Devi.
  • Wife: profiter of own disobedience; equity bars.
  • Decision: Divorce GRANTED — mere disinclination ≠ wrong.
Ratio: 13(1A) open to guilty party; wrong needs grave positive misconduct. Exam: “grant” half of 13(1A) answers.
Case 09 — T. Srinivasan v. T. Varalakshmi, Mad HC aff. AIR 1999 SC 595 (DU p.161) — weaponised RCR = wrong
FactsHusband took ex-parte RCR decree; drove wife away 23-5-1977; police writing; paper-trail notices Ex.A-4/5/6/8; sought 13(1A)(ii). Trial granted; Mad HC reversed; SC affirmed.
  • Issues: (1) Engineered non-compliance = wrong? (2) Notices prove bona fides?
  • Husband: Dharmendra; 1 yr elapsed; notices show willingness.
  • Wife: decree as device; driven away; notices manufactured; Hirachand.
  • Decision: DENIED — positive wrong; Dharmendra distinguished.
Ratio: engineering non-compliance + violence = wrong; paper willingness belied by conduct. Exam: “deny” half with Hirachand.
Case 10 — Hirachand v. Sunanda, (2001) 4 SCC 125 (DU p.168) — continuing adultery + default = wrong
FactsWife held JS on adultery + Rs.100 maintenance; husband continued with mistress + wilfully defaulted (“marking time”); sought 13(1A)(i). Trial granted; HC reversed; SC appeal.
  • Issues: (1) Post-JS adultery = continuing wrong? (2) Maintenance default = wrong? (3) Dharmendra scope?
  • Husband: Dharmendra; either party; tie suspended.
  • Wife: tie subsists (Sec 10(2)); continuing offence; marking time; Soundarammal.
  • Decision: DENIED — positive misconduct; disapproved Sumitra Manna, Bai Mani.
Ratio: post-decree adultery + wilful default = wrong; Sec 23(1)(a) ethical filter on post-decree conduct. Exam: “deny” half; adultery-continuing point.
Case 11 — Sureshta Devi v. Om Prakash, AIR 1991 SC 190 (DU p.174) — consent withdrawable
FactsJoint 13B petition; wife withdrew before second motion (pressure/terms); husband pressed one-sided decree. HC conflict (Bom/Del/MP allowed). SC reference.
  • Issues: (1) Must consent subsist till decree? (2) Unilateral withdrawal allowed? (3) O23 R1 analogy?
  • Husband: first-motion consent suffices; withdrawal mala fide; Bom/Del/MP views.
  • Wife: “not withdrawn in meantime”; 6–18 mo = second-thought period; consent must be free + subsisting.
  • Decision: NO decree — withdrawal valid; overruled Bom/Del/MP.
Ratio: 13B needs subsisting mutual consent till decree; either may withdraw. Exam: every 13B-withdrawal PYQ (Maya/Anil).
Case 12 — Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur, (2017) 8 SCC 746 (supp.) — cooling waivable
Facts8+ yrs separation; joint 13B; sought waiver of 6-mo cooling; settlement complete, mediation failed. Question: mandatory? who waives (Manish Goel Art-142-only?)
  • Issues: (1) 13B(2) mandatory or directory? (2) Family Court or only SC? (3) Conditions?
  • Parties (joint): directory (Kailash v. Nanhku); Family Court can waive; reunion impossible.
  • Objector-side: Manish Goel; cooling protects reconciliation; Sureshta sanctity.
  • Decision: WAIVER allowed — directory; Family Court may waive on 4 conditions.
Ratio: 4 conditions — 13B(1) done + 18 mo total, mediation failed, all settled, agony-prolongation; guided discretion. Exam: every waiver PYQ + procedure drafting.
