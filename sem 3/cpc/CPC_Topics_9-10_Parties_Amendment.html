1. PARTIES TO SUITS — Order I (Rr.1–13) JOINDER • NECESSARY v PROPER • MISJOINDER v NON-JOINDER • ADD/IMPLEAD • OBJECTION & WAIVER

BARE ACT Order I deals with who may/must be parties, joinder, representative suits, and cures for wrong joinder. It also touches joinder of causes (with O.2) and representative suits (R.8). Question of joinder arises only when act done by/affecting 2+ persons (A assaults B = no joinder; A assaults B+C or A+B assault C = joinder).

★ O.1 R.1 — WHO MAY BE JOINED AS PLAINTIFFS (1976 SUBSTITUTION) “All persons may be joined in one suit as plaintiffs where— (a) any right to relief in respect of, or arising out of, the same act or transaction or series of acts or transactions is alleged to exist in such persons, whether jointly, severally or in the alternative; and (b) if such persons brought separate suits, any common question of law or fact would arise.”Two cumulative conditions joined by “and” — both must be satisfied (Stroud v. Lawson; Sudhansu Sekhar; Krishna Laxman). Object: avoid multiplicity + expenses (Iswar Bhai v. Harihar Behera).
★ O.1 R.2 — SEPARATE TRIAL (PLAINTIFFS) “Where it appears that joinder of plaintiffs may embarrass or delay trial, Court may put plaintiffs to election or order separate trials or make such other order as expedient.”
★ O.1 R.3 — WHO MAY BE JOINED AS DEFENDANTS (1976) + R.3A “All persons may be joined as defendants where— (a) any right to relief … arising out of same act/transaction/series … is alleged to exist against such persons, jointly/severally/alternatively; and (b) if separate suits were brought, any common question of law or fact would arise.”
R.3A (1976): If joinder of defendants may embarrass/delay trial, Court may order separate trials or other expedient order in interests of justice.“And” = cumulative (Stroud; Sant Singh v. Deo Ram; Govindaraja FB). Object: avoid multiplicity (Iswar Bhai; State Bank of Patiala).
★ O.1 Rr.4–7 — SUPPLEMENTARY JOINDER RULES R.4: Judgment may be given without amendment for such plaintiffs entitled / against such defendants liable according to respective liabilities.
R.5: Defendant need not be interested in all relief claimed.
R.6: Plaintiff may at option join all/any persons severally or jointly+severally liable on one contract (incl. bills/hundis/promissory notes).
R.7: Where plaintiff in doubt from whom redress due, may join 2+ defendants so liability/extent determined inter se.

1.1 Twin Conditions + Illustrations (Takwani — memorise 2 each)

RuleCondition (i): same act/transactionCondition (ii): common Q of law/factIllustrations
R.1 PlaintiffsRight to relief in each plaintiff arises from same act/transaction/seriesSeparate suits would raise common Q✅ Joint oil contract: A agrees jointly with B+C to sell 100 tins, refuses — B+C sue jointly (same breach + common Q). ✅ Simultaneous assault: A assaults B+C together — joint suit. ❌ Separate oil contracts: A contracts separately with B and C (same day/price) — different transactions → cannot join.
R.3 DefendantsRight against them from same act/transaction/seriesSeparate suits would raise common Q✅ Bus-car collision injuring passer-by A: join bus owner B + car owner C (same collision + common negligence Q; R.7 doubt also). ✅ Joint assault: B+C assault A — join both. ❌ 4 separate oil contracts (B,C,D,E with A): 4 transactions → cannot join.
⚠️ DOUBLE MISJOINDER

If neither plaintiffs nor defendants jointly interested and no common transaction/Q — suit bad for double misjoinder (misjoinder of plaintiffs + causes AND defendants + causes). Also see O.2 Rr.3–7 (joinder of causes). Court may order separate trials (Rr.2, 3A) instead of dismissing.

1.2 Necessary Party v Proper Party — The Core Distinction (2 tests)

FC/SC definition (Atiqa Begum; Udit Narain Singh; Ramesh Hirachand; Kasturi v. Iyyamperumal): “Necessary party = presence indispensable to constitution of suit, against whom relief sought, without whom no effective order can be passed. Proper party = effective order possible without, but presence necessary for complete/final decision (‘effectually and completely’).” In absence of necessary → no decree; in absence of proper → decree as to parties before court, but adjudication incomplete.

Two tests (Hardeva v. Ismail FB; Kasturi; Benares Bank FB — quote): (1) Right to some relief against such party in respect of matter involved; AND (2) Effective decree impossible in absence. Both must concur for “necessary”.

Necessary party (examples)Proper party (examples)
• All sharers in partition suit
• Auction-purchaser in suit to set aside public auction
• Selected/appointed candidates in challenge to selection (directly affected — Ranjeet Mal; Prabodh Verma)
• Co-owner where joint right inseparable (subject to proof — Laxmi Shankar; Vishwanath Pillai)		• Subtenant in landlord v tenant possession suit
• Grandsons in sons v father partition
• Local authority (beneficiary) in land-acquisition reference (UP Awas v. Gyan Devi)
• Person with fair semblance of title seeking impleadment (Md. Sabir Ansari); administrator-suit transacting parties (Babulal Khandelwal)
✍️ EXAM TRAP: CO-OWNER SUITS

Pal Singh v. Sunder Singh; Laxmi Shankar v. Yash Ram; Vishwanath Pillai: one co-owner can maintain eviction/possession vs stranger if other co-owners do not object; vague plea of non-joinder without evidence (pedigree/revenue record) fails — cannot dismiss for non-joinder without proof of necessary party. B.P. Rao (1985 Supp): where absent persons’ interests identical + sufficiently represented by parties before court → not dismissed.

1.3 Misjoinder v Non-joinder + Effect (O.1 R.9 + Proviso, 1976)

★ O.1 R.9 — MISJOINDER AND NON-JOINDER (VERBATIM) “No suit shall be defeated by reason of the misjoinder or non-joinder of parties, and the Court may in every suit deal with the matter in controversy so far as regards the rights and interests of the parties actually before it: Provided that nothing in this rule shall apply to non-joinder of a necessary party.”1976 proviso added — general saving rule does NOT save absence of necessary party.
HeadNon-joinderMisjoinder
MeaningNecessary/proper person not joined though should be2+ persons joined as plaintiffs/defendants in contravention of R.1/R.3, being neither necessary nor proper
Sub-typesNon-joinder of necessary (fatal) vs proper (not fatal)Misjoinder of plaintiffs (no same transaction/common Q) / defendants (reverse) / double
EffectNecessary → suit liable to dismissal (no effective decree); Proper → court may proceed for parties before it or add under R.10Not fatal — court deals with parties before it; may order separate trials (Rr.2/3A), strike out (R.10(2))
ObjectionMust be taken at earliest, at/before settlement of issues (R.13), else waived — except ground subsequently arising; but necessary-party defect goes to root — court may still notice.

1.4 Cure — Addition/Substitution/Striking Out (O.1 R.10) + Limitation (S.21)

★ O.1 R.10 — SUIT IN WRONG PLAINTIFF + ADD/STRIKE (SUMMARY) R.10(1) Wrong plaintiff: Suit in name of wrong person or doubt as to right plaintiff → Court at any stage, if bona fide mistake + necessary for real matter, may substitute/add plaintiff on just terms. Ill.: C (agent of A) sues B in own name by bona fide mistake → substitute A.
R.10(2) Strike/add: At any stage, suo motu/on application, on just terms — strike out improperly joined (plaintiff/defendant) AND add person who ought to have been joined OR whose presence necessary to effectually+completely adjudicate all questions. Two alternative grounds.
R.10(3): No person added as plaintiff / next friend without consent (cannot force litigation).
R.10(4): Where defendant added → amend plaint (unless court directs otherwise) + serve amended summons+plaint on new (+ if fit, original) defendant.
R.10(5): Subject to Limitation Act S.22 (now S.21, 1963), proceedings vs added defendant deemed begun only on service of summons.
Rr.11–12: Court may give conduct of suit to proper person; one of several plaintiffs/defendants may authorise another in writing to appear/plead/act.
  • When R.10(2): (i) ought to have been joined (necessary/proper), OR (ii) presence necessary for complete decision. Court looks for fair semblance of title/interest, not success chances (Md. Sabir). Allowed at any stage incl. appeal, on terms.
  • S.21 Limitation link (PYQ): S.21(1): added/substituted plaintiff/defendant → suit deemed instituted when made party (limitation runs till then). Proviso: if omission due to good-faith mistake, court may direct relation-back to earlier date (Ram Prasad v. Vijay Kumar; Karuppaswamy v. Ramamurthy — dead defendant, prompt bona fide action → relate back). S.21(2) exceptions (no fresh limitation): assignment/devolution, plaintiff↔defendant transposition, purchaser pendente lite stepping into shoes (K.S. Lokaiah). S.21 only addition, not transposition; only suits, not execution.
  • Jai Jai Ram link: misdescription correction under O.1 R.10 / S.153 / O.6 R.17 relates back to original institution (not S.21 new party) — no limitation issue.
★ O.1 R.13 — OBJECTIONS AS TO NON-JOINDER/MISJOINDER (VERBATIM) “All objections on ground of non-joinder or misjoinder shall be taken at earliest possible opportunity and, where issues settled, at or before such settlement, unless ground subsequently arose, and any such objection not so taken shall be deemed waived.”With O.2 R.7 (misjoinder of causes) — early-objection + waiver policy; prevents ambush after trial.
📝 O.1 R.8 REPRESENTATIVE SUIT (CONTEXT — 1 PARA)

Where numerous persons, same interest — one+ may sue/defend for all with permission/direction; court gives notice at plaintiff’s expense (personal/advertisement); any interested may apply to be added; no abandon/withdraw/compromise without notice; non-diligent representative may be substituted; decree binding on all. Explanation: same interest ≠ same cause of action. Bhupendra Singh v. Municipal Council. R.8A: court may permit amicus-type opinion on Q of law in public interest.

2. AMENDMENT OF PLEADINGS — Order VI, Rule 17 (+ Proviso, R.18) OBJECT • AT ANY STAGE • DUE DILIGENCE • ALLOW v REFUSE • LIMITATION • PLAINT v WRITTEN STATEMENT

BARE ACT Pleading = plaint or written statement (O.6 R.1; Mogha: written statements of contentions + details opponent needs). Object (Throp v. Holdsworth, Jessel MR; Sayad Muhammad, Halsbury; Ganesh Trading): bring parties to definite issues, narrow conflict, prevent surprise/enlargement, diminish expense/delay, guide trial/burden/evidence/relief. O.6 R.2: concise material facts, not evidence/law (plead facts not law; mixed Q plead; material = primary facts to prove cause/defence — Udhav Singh; Virender Nath; particulars = amplification). R.7 (Departure): no new/inconsistent ground except by amendment. R.16: strike out unnecessary/scandalous/frivolous/vexatious/prejudicing/abusing matter. Rr.17–18 govern amendment.

★ O.6 R.17 — AMENDMENT OF PLEADINGS (2002 SUBSTITUTION — VERBATIM, MEMORISE) “The Court may at any stage of the proceedings allow either party to alter or amend his pleadings in such manner and on such terms as may be just, and all such amendments shall be made as may be necessary for the purpose of determining the real questions in controversy between the parties: Provided that no application for amendment shall be allowed after the trial has commenced, unless the Court comes to the conclusion that in spite of due diligence, the party could not have raised the matter before the commencement of trial.”“May” = discretionary, judicial. Pre-2002: pure “at any stage”; 2002 proviso adds due-diligence bar post-commencement (applies from 1-7-2002).
★ O.6 R.18 — FAILURE TO AMEND AFTER ORDER “If party obtaining leave does not amend within time limited or 14 days (if no time), he shall not be permitted after unless time extended.”With S.148 extension + costs; prevents stale leaves.

2.1 Object — Handmaid, Not Punishment (DU + Universal + Takwani — quote 1 line in every answer)

“Rules of procedure are intended to be a handmaid to administration of justice. A party cannot be refused just relief merely because of mistake, negligence, inadvertence or even infraction of procedure. Court always gives leave unless mala fide or opponent injured beyond costs. However negligent/late, allow if without injustice.” — Jai Jai Ram (Shah J.), quoting Amulakchand; Ganesh Trading (Beg CJ.): “Procedural law to facilitate, not obstruct substantive justice; pleadings to give intimation, determine real issue, prevent deviation.”
“Object of courts is to decide rights, not punish mistakes… Courts don’t exist for discipline but for deciding matters; amendment as of right if without injustice.” — Cropper/Cooper v. Smith, Bowen LJ (DU Universal). “All amendments ought to be allowed satisfying (a) no injustice + (b) necessary for real Q.” — Kisandas v. Vithoba (DU Universal).
  • Dominant purpose (PYQ): try merits, prevent multiplicity, minimise litigation, do complete justice (court may allow even if relief within limitation; costs compensate).
  • Ganga Bai caution (DU BK Pillai): power wide, at any stage, limitation notwithstanding — but far-reaching discretion governed by judicial considerations; wider discretion → greater care/circumspection.

2.2 “At Any Stage” + 2002 Proviso — Due Diligence (most tested after 2002)

  • “At any stage” (pre-proviso scope): before/at/after trial, first appeal, second appeal, revision, Supreme Court, even execution (if decree legal/enforceable) — not governed by limitation as such. Trial court ordinarily grants; appellate/revisional/SC also can.
  • Proviso (2002) bar: after trial commenced (issues framed + evidence begins — filing affidavit/examination), no amendment unless court concludes due diligence despite, matter could not be raised before commencement. Van Vibhag (2011): specific-performance plea after 11 years → refused. South Konkan (2009): extremely liberal if refusal = irreparable loss and allows ultimate justice / avoids further litigation — proviso applied with justice-lens.
  • Q.3/2020 OBE (25m) + Q.2(a)/2018: firm-name correction even after trial — misdescription clarification, diligence shown (prompt on objection), no new case → allow (Jai Jai Ram); proviso satisfied or inapplicable (not new matter, only proper description).
  • Rajesh Kumar Aggarwal v. K.K. Modi (2006): restates R.17 + proviso + object (merits + no injustice/prejudice).

2.3 Two Cardinal Tests (Kisandas — apply to every problem)

TestQuestion to askIf YES/NO
(a) Necessary for real Q?Is amendment necessary to determine real questions in controversy (not merely technical/useless)?YES → favour allow; NO → refuse (Mulla ground 1).
(b) Without injustice?Can it be allowed without injustice to other side (compensable by costs/other terms + opportunity to meet)?YES → allow (even if negligent/late — Clarpede); NO (accrued right lost / wholly displaced / mala fide) → refuse.

Clarpede v. Commercial Union: negligence/carelessness/lateness immaterial if compensable in money — no injustice. Court applies judicial mind, hears both sides; wholly discretionary.

2.4 When Leave REFUSED — Mulla 5 + Takwani 4 + Dalip 9 (learn one list, cite others)

Mulla 5 (PYQ Q.2(a)/2019 — write verbatim)

  1. Not necessary for real Q (merely technical/useless/no substance).
  2. Plaintiff’s suit wholly displaced by proposed amendment (Steward v. North Metropolitan Tramways, 1886).
  3. Takes away legal right accrued by lapse of time (Weldon v. Neal).
  4. Introduces totally different, new, inconsistent case at belated stage.
  5. Application not in good faith.

Takwani 4 (same, with Pollock test + English law)

  1. Not necessary for real Q.
  2. Wholly displaces / new-inconsistent / changes fundamental character — Steward (tramway liability shifted to local authority after limitation vs authority expired; had it been pleaded earlier plaintiff could have sued authority → refused). Pollock B test (quote): “Whether defendants can amend without placing plaintiff in position he cannot be recouped by costs/otherwise. Here action wholly displaced → ought not be allowed.” Straight J.: cannot fail on one case then succeed on antagonistic other.
  3. Accrued right by time — Weldon v. Neal (1887) (slander → add assault/false imprisonment after limitation → refused). Not universal: limitation is one factor, not power-ouster (Ganga Bai; Arundhati Mishra); Charan Das v. Amir Khan (Buckmaster — quote): “Full power cannot be disputed; as rule not exercised to take accrued right, yet cases where outweighed by special circumstances.”
  4. Mala fide — no substantial ground / defeat-delay / reagitate / afterthought to avert inevitable (Pirgonda; Jai Jai Ram; Patasibai; Kanakarathanammal).

Dalip Kaur 9 principles (DU p.67 — P&H synthesis of AIR 1967/1974/1978 — quote in full)

  1. Allow all necessary for real controversies.
  2. Not alter/substitute cause of action of original lis.
  3. No inconsistent/contradictory in negation of admitted facts or mutually destructive allegations.
  4. No prejudice uncompensable by costs.
  5. No time-barred claim/relief.
  6. No defeating accrued legal right by lapse.
  7. No suffering on technicalities; minimise litigation.
  8. Delay compensated by costs.
  9. Non-fraudulent error/mistake ≠ ground to reject.
✍️ “WELL-SETTLED RULE” COMMENT (Q.4(b)/2017 10m — ONE PARA)

“All amendments should be permitted as necessary for determining real question in controversy, unless injustice results.” Illustrates Jai Jai Ram/Purushottam/Amulakchand rule: handmaid, merits over technicality, costs cure negligence/lateness, but bars wholly-displacing (Steward/Pollock), accrued-right (Weldon/Charan Das), new-inconsistent, mala fide amendments; delay→costs; error→allow. See §2.1–2.4 + cases.

2.5 Cause of Action Doctrine — New Case vs Additional Approach (A.K. Gupta — heart of Ganesh/BK Pillai)

“General rule: no new case/new cause of action, especially if suit on new case time-barred (Weldon v. Neal). But where amendment is no more than different/additional approach to same facts, allow even after limitation (Charan Das; L.J. Leach v. Jardine Skinner). Reasons: (1) courts decide rights, not punish mistakes (Cropper v. Smith); (2) party cannot rely on limitation when substance already in pleading (Kisandas; Pirgonda).” — A.K. Gupta v. Damodar Valley, AIR 1967 SC 96 (majority), quoted DU Ganesh para 9–10 + BK Pillai para 4.
“‘Cause of action’ ≠ every material fact (Cooke v. Gill). For amendment, new claim on new basis constituted by new facts (Robinson v. Unicos); ‘new case’ = new set of ideas (Dornan). No amendment introducing new ideas to prejudice right acquired by lapse.” — A.K. Gupta p.98.
  • Ganesh gloss (Beg CJ., DU p.63): cause = whole bundle of essential facts antecedent to suit; mere failure to set out essential fact ≠ new cause; defective pleadings curable if cause not ab initio completely absent (even very defective → cure with court-fee/costs); refuse ordinarily only if lapse bars newly-constituted cause.
  • Applications: adding money-recovery to declaratory suit (A.K. Gupta) → allowed (same contract, additional approach); adding title history to O.21 R.103 suit (Pirgonda) → allowed (no new case, no surprise, costs); adding dissolution facts to clarify capacity (Ganesh) → allowed; adding alternative S.60(b) plea to WS (BK Pillai) → allowed (extension of plaintiff’s plea, no admission withdrawn).
  • Puran Ram v. Bhaguram (2008): correcting property description in specific-performance suit (S.26 Specific Relief proviso permits) → no limitation/character change, no fresh suit needed.

2.6 Plaint v Written Statement — Defendant More Favoured + Admission Rule

  • Same principles, more generous to WS: prejudice less likely; defendant may take alternative/inconsistent pleas (lease + licence-irrevocable) unless injustice or admission withdrawn (BK Pillai).
  • Admission withdrawal bar — Heeralal v. Kalyan Mal, AIR 1998 SC 618 (DU Universal): once WS admits in plaintiff’s favour, cannot withdraw by amendment if it wholly displaces plaintiff’s case + irretrievable prejudice. Modi Spg. v. Ladha Ram; Ishaq v. Iqbal; Suraj Prakash; Jagan Nath — same.
  • PYQ tenant (Q.2(a)/2019): B admits tenancy + paid without receipt → seeks to deny tenancy → withdraws admission + wholly displaces A’s rent suit + inconsistent/mutually destructive → refuse (Steward/Pollock + Heeralal + Mulla 2/4).
  • BK Pillai distinction: lessee plea + alternative “if licensee then irrevocable S.60(b) + limitation + permanent works” → neither inconsistent nor admission withdrawn, only extension/rebuttal to eviction issue → allow with arrears + Rs.3000 costs. Delay alone ≠ refuse if compensable.
  • Narinder Singh (DLT): specific-performance plaintiff adding lease-extension relief to save infructuous suit, taking away termination right → refuse.

2.7 Procedure — Who, When, How (Takwani (j)–(n) + Universal)

  • Who may apply: plaintiff/defendant (one+ of several) for own pleading; plaintiff for replication to set-off; additional WS with leave.
  • Who may grant: ordinarily trial court; also appellate/revisional/SC in appropriate case.
  • Notice + hearing opposite: must hear objection; grant without hearing illegal (except purely formal/technical). Serve amended plaint on defendant.
  • Merits not judged: at amendment stage, correctness/falsity not decided — only allowability.
  • Reasons + terms: record reasons; impose costs + opportunity to meet (consequential amendment/replication, additional issues, recall).
  • Subsequent events: generally suit decided on facts at institution, but court may notice subsequent events to shorten litigation / protect rights / do justice and allow amendment.
  • R.18 + appeal/revision/writ: amend in time/14 days else leave lapses (extendable); amendment order interlocutory — revision only if S.115 proviso satisfied; writ/227 exceptional (Haridas Alidas v. Godrej cut-off in Universal).

4A. Jai Jai Ram Manohar Lal v. National Building Material Supply Co., AIR 1969 SC 1267 — J.C. Shah J.

HYPOTHETICAL VISUAL — JAI JAI RAM: BUSINESS NAME → REAL MAN (MISDESCRIPTION, NOT NULLITY)

📜 Facts (DU pp.58–59, paras 1–4)

11.3.1950 — SUIT (SUB-JUDGE NAINITAL) Manohar Lal s/o Jai Jai Ram sues National Building Material Supply, Gurgaon for Rs.10,139/12 (timber supplied). Action in business name “Jai Jai Ram Manohar Lal” (commission agency); signed/verified “by pen of Manohar Lal”. Defendant: plaintiff unregistered firm → incompetent to sue.

❓ Issues

  1. Whether suit in business name was by non-existent person (nullity) or misdescription of existing person (curable)?
  2. Whether amendment requires express bona fide-mistake averment, and whether it relates back or prospective + time-barred?
  3. Scope of O.6 R.17 / S.153 and Purushottam/Amulakchand applicability?

⚖️ Arguments

Plaintiff Manohar LalDefendant National Building + HC view
• Real plaintiff always Manohar (HUF manager, competent); business name misdescription; amendment merely proper description for real Q.
• No bona fide-averment rule; power serves ends, not narrow technicality.
• Relates back to 1950 — no limitation issue; defendant persisted in meritless plea → costs.		• Non-existing person / unregistered firm cannot sue; nullity cannot be amended into person.
• No bona fide plea → incompetent; should be Karta/all members (Purushottam).
• Amendment adds new plaintiff 18.7.52 → time-barred (limitation accrued).

📖 Decision (Shah J., DU pp.59–61, paras 5–9)

  • Appeal allowed; HC set aside; trial amendment restored; remanded to HC on merits; defendant pays costs (HC + SC) for meritless persistence.
  • Handmaid quote (para 5) + Beaumont test (Amulakchand, AIR 1933 Bom 304): “Non-existent → nullity, no cure; misdescription → prima facie amend, loss compensable by costs.” Amulakchand: HUF firm-name suit → substitute 3 members → ought to have been allowed (not O.30 case).
  • Purushottam Umedbhai (para 6, p.994): firm outside India suing in firm name (no O.30 privilege) = misdescribing partners collectively, not nullity; plaint by all partners with defective description; amend under S.153 (possibly O.6 R.17 — no opinion) to assist real Q. 💡 Ratio (quote)

“Misdescription of existing plaintiff (business/firm name for real manager/partners) is curable by amendment relating back; only suit by truly non-existent person is nullity. PYQ 1 — Q.2(a)/2019: A sues B for 2 years’ rent (B tenant). B admits tenancy, pleads paid without receipt. During trial B seeks to amend WS to deny tenancy. Decide.

★ BEST MODEL ANSWER (10-mark)

Provision (2m): Quote O.6 R.17 + proviso verbatim. General rule: material facts in pleadings, no decision outside pleadings, but illiteracy/counsel gaps necessitate amendment with court permission. Object: try merits, real Q, complete justice, prevent multiplicity (courts exist for justice not punishment).

Allow if (1m): (1) necessary for real Q, (2) without injustice, (3) subserves ultimate justice/avoids further litigation.

Refuse — Mulla 5 (2m): (1) unnecessary/technical, (2) wholly displaces plaintiff’s suit, (3) takes accrued limitation right, (4) new/inconsistent/belated, (5) mala fide.
